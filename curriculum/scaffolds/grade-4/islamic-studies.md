## Proposed culminating performance

**Candidate task:** Use attributed sources to create a two-event early-community timeline and explain the difference between an event and an ethical interpretation.

**Assessment gap:** Author actual tasks, citations, administration conditions, permitted supports, teacher keys or model answers and objective success criteria. Check accurate representation, evidence-based reasoning and clear explanation. **Do not grade private faith, sincerity or worship participation.** A proposed exercise is neither an authenticated juristic ruling nor a learner result.

## Trivium in this subject

- **Grammar:** name and accurately use selected Arabic/English terms; distinguish Qur'an, hadith, historical narration, translation and scholarly commentary.
- **Logic:** test source attribution and inference, preserve unknown authenticity, and compare competing explanations fairly.
- **Rhetoric:** give a respectful, checkable account grounded in reviewed and explicitly cited sources.

## Investigation 2 scaffold — Ramadan and community care

**Status.** Proposed sequence with original, fictional rehearsal prompts and draft scoring criteria. Actual religious source material, teaching approval, and course and outcome IDs are pending. The [K–12 matrix](../README.md) includes Islamic studies in every grade. The [course index](../../README.md) distinguishes the registered Grade 2 mathematics draft from the unregistered Islamic studies pilots. Grade 2 introduces the five pillars and names fasting. The [Grade 3 pilot](../../islamic-studies/grade-3-source-literacy-pilot/README.md) teaches source classification and claim checking. This investigation uses those skills to weigh evidence and explain a community decision.

### Candidate source and teaching boundary

Use [Qur’an 2:185](https://quran.com/2/185) as the candidate locator for qualified review. The draft reading question asks how the verse connects Ramadan with revelation and fasting, and which circumstances it explicitly mentions when discussing fasting. Expected evidence after review includes the connection to revelation and the references to illness and travel. The teacher should distinguish that textual observation from a ruling about an individual and from the local event rules. Select the Arabic edition and attributed translation during review. Keep the source card as a locator until the wording, access and reuse are approved.

### Entry and progression rules

Use three untimed prompts with a new, neutral library example. Read aloud or supply a printed copy and accept pointing, speech or writing.

| Check | Prompt and expected evidence | If evidence is incomplete |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R1 | A dated library notice says the room opens at noon. A pupil's note predicts it will be crowded. Point to the record and identify the prediction. Require both distinctions. | Model with two labeled strips, then recheck with a different notice and prediction. |
| R2 | Which line supports “the room opens at noon”? Require the notice and its opening-time detail. | Match a claim to a highlighted line during practice. Fade highlighting for a fresh check. |
| R3 | Give a reason for leaving a clear walkway when the library rearranges tables. Require an action and a plausible reason tied to access. | Model “I suggest ___ because ___,” then check a different access situation with the same blank frame. |

Start guided work after R1 and R2 are demonstrated. R3 may be taught alongside guided work and must be demonstrated before the independent transfer. Retain read-aloud, glossary, blank frames and response-mode supports during checks. Record only independently demonstrated skills for advancement. A missing skill leads to targeted reteaching and a fresh equivalent check. Grade placement, Arabic fluency and a prior course enrollment are unnecessary as entry evidence.

### Learning objectives

| Local label | Observable objective |
| --- | --- |
| L1 | Distinguish an attributed religious source or its labeled translation, a local notice and a learner inference. Identify a claim supported by the reviewed religious source and explain one limit of what each card can establish. |
| L2 | Compare two fictional Ramadan event plans against two stated access or privacy constraints. Point to the evidence and identify a claim the evidence cannot settle. |
| L3 | Recommend or revise a plan in a short explanation with a claim, relevant evidence and a reason. Identify a decision that belongs with a qualified adult. |

| Episode | Grammar | Logic | Rhetoric and teacher check |

| 1. Read across sources | Teach Ramadan, fasting, generosity, privacy and the labels source, translation and notice. Prepare a reviewed Qur'an passage on fasting and a separately labeled translation. | Sort a passage card, a fictional event notice and a pupil's recommendation. Ask which can establish the event start time and why. | Learner explains one boundary to a partner. The notice gives the fictional schedule. A recommendation needs its own reason. Use placeholder source metadata for rehearsal until review. |
| 2. Compare plans | Mark the words “all stations” and “optional” in two fictional plans. | Given a step-free access requirement and an optional participation rule, compare Plan A, which uses a stairs-only room and requires everyone to pack kits, with Plan B, which uses a step-free room and makes packing optional. | Learner recommends B and points to both requirements. Ask whether the notice tells us what any pupil should do about fasting. Key: the notice governs the event. An individual question goes privately to an appropriate adult. |
| 3. Transfer independently | Identify the source type of each card and define one access or privacy term in context. | Apply both constraints to a fresh fictional event and identify an unsupported claim. | Give an oral, written or diagram-supported recommendation with a reason and a private route for questions. Present the unit as Islamic Studies grounded in the Trivium. The school-wide Quadrivium continues through arithmetic, geometry and observational astronomy. This investigation selects no mathematical art. Calendar mentions serve as context. Keep scenarios fictional. Invite no disclosures about health, fasting, worship or family practice. Route specific health questions to a parent or guardian and a qualified health professional as appropriate, and religious rulings to an appropriate qualified religious educator.

### Independent evidence and acceptance

Use a fresh fictional Ramadan drive notice that requires step-free access and allows learners to contribute privately. Option A uses a step-free room and a public sign-up. Option B uses a step-free room and private sign-up. Include the reviewed source card and an invented learner claim that the event notice settles who must fast. Ask learners to label the cards, state one idea supported by the reviewed reading, compare both options, recommend a plan and explain where to take an individual fasting question.

- **L1:** Correctly label all three cards and state one valid limit for each. The learner identifies one relevant idea grounded in the approved reading, such as the connection between Ramadan and revelation, and explains that the verse card supplies no local event logistics. The notice sets local logistics. The learner claim requires support.
- **L2:** Explain that both plans meet the access rule and only B meets the privacy rule. Identify the individual fasting claim as unsupported by the notice.
- **L3:** Recommend B, or propose a feasible revision of A that satisfies both constraints. Cite a relevant notice detail, connect it to the recommendation and identify a qualified adult for a private follow-up.
- Use no speed threshold. Require evidence for each objective separately. Record outcomes privately, give targeted feedback and use a fresh equivalent prompt for reassessment.
- Before release, a qualified reviewer must verify the exact passage locator, Arabic text, translation attribution and permissions, fiqh boundaries and age suitability. A curriculum reviewer must check the entry probes, accessibility, item/key agreement and available teaching time. Rehearse the rubric using one complete response and one response missing each objective. Each omission must be detected independently.
- Future digital forms should generate fresh fictional scenarios and teacher keys from the stated constraints, with both options and the correct response position varying. A resumed session must replay its saved form and key. These are acceptance criteria for later tooling.

### Parallel form for reassessment

Use this fictional form as a teacher rehearsal and as a blueprint for later generated forms. Use the same reviewed source card and access supports as in the first form. Give feedback on the first attempt before administering a fresh form independently.

**Learner cards.** A Ramadan book drive notice requires a step-free drop-off and says donor names must stay private. Plan A sets out an anonymous drop box at the step-free entrance. Plan B sets out an anonymous drop box on a floor reachable only by stairs. A learner writes, “The book drive notice tells us which pupils must fast.” The teacher supplies the approved reading card separately.

**Prompt.** Name what kind of evidence each card supplies and one limit of each. State one idea supported by the reading. Compare both plans against both notice rules. Say whether the learner's claim is supported. Recommend a plan or revision, explain your reason using the notice, and say where you could take an individual fasting question privately.

**Teacher key.** Apply the same L1 criteria as on the first form. For L2, both plans protect donor names and only A meets the access rule. The notice supplies no evidence about who must fast. For L3, accept A or a feasible revision that moves B to a step-free location while retaining anonymity. Require a relevant notice detail, an explanation that connects it to the recommendation, and a private referral to a qualified religious educator for the religious question.

| Rehearsal response | Expected scoring |
| --- | --- |
| Complete response with correct source labels, limits and a grounded reading idea, both comparisons, the unsupported claim, and a justified recommendation with private referral | L1, L2 and L3 demonstrated |
| Complete except for the idea supported by the reading | L1 evidence missing |
| Complete except for comparing one of the two options against the access rule | L2 evidence missing |
| Complete except for the private referral | L3 evidence missing |

Both forms require the same evidence and use the same supports. The unmet requirement switches from privacy to access, and the compliant option switches from B to A. For generated forms, derive the key from each plan's access and privacy properties and require exactly one compliant option. Preserve an accepted revision as a separate response path under L3. Save the form and its derived key together for exact session replay.
