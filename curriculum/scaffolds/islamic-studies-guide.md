# Islamic studies — K–12 source and curriculum guide

- Status: **Islamic studies selected for K–12 planning; individual course scopes still unreviewed**
- Record kind: subject-planning guidance, not a course, lesson, adopted schedule or assessment
- Course and outcome identifiers: **unassigned**
- Grade-specific proposals: [K–12 matrix](README.md)

## Proposed strands

| Strand | Early grades (K–3) | Middle grades (4–8) | Later grades (9–12) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Qur'an and Arabic vocabulary | Listening, accurately named surahs and basic meaning | Translation, context, interpretation and direct quotations | Exegetical methods, textual transmission and research |
| 'Aqīdah and akhlāq | Introductory beliefs, kindness, gratitude and honesty | Ethical responsibilities and respectful reasons | Theology, ethics and documented scholarly differences |
| Worship and fiqh | Naming and describing practices | Evidence for practices, conditions and different juristic positions | Uṣūl al-fiqh, school-specific arguments and limits of inference |
| Sīrah and Islamic history | Sourced prophet stories and event sequences | The early community and connected historical settings | Primary/secondary source criticism and historical disputes |
| Hadith studies | A narrated report versus Qur'anic text | Matn, isnād, attribution and what remains unknown | Narrators, chains, defects, authenticity grades and evidence limits |

These are **planning hypotheses**, not standardized religious rulings, instructional mandates or attainment claims. Qur'anic memorization, tajwīd, Arabic fluency and practical worship instruction would require separately reviewed goals, access, materials and assessment conditions.

## Source custody and verification

1. For Qur'anic passages, record the **surah and ayah** and the exact translation or tafsīr used. Distinguish Arabic primary text from translation and commentary.
2. For each hadith, record a traceable collection and report identifier/edition. Distinguish **isnād** (reported transmission chain) from **matn** (reported content). A cited report is not automatically authenticated. Support any claimed ṣaḥīḥ, ḥasan or ḍaʿīf assessment with its critical source, or mark it **not yet verified**.
3. When a legal position is involved, identify the juristic school or scholar and the supporting sources. Compare established differences respectfully; do not turn an exploratory task into a fatwa or falsely assert universal agreement.
4. Source and date Islamic historical claims where possible, distinguishing Qur'anic statements, hadith reports, later sīrah, scholarly interpretations and a learner's inference.
5. Attribute and review external readings, access requirements and reuse rights under the [resource-readiness policy](../../docs/curriculum-model.md). No public-domain status or permission to copy is assumed.

## Trivium and assessment boundaries

- **Grammar:** sound terminology, text identification and careful citation.
- **Logic:** source reliability, inference, assumptions, possible defects and legitimate disagreements.
- **Rhetoric:** a clear, well-sourced explanation that treats alternative interpretations fairly.
- Measure *knowledge, textual understanding, reasoning and communication* rather than **private faith, sincerity, worship attendance or family practice**.
- Keep individual evidence out of this repository. Use qualified teachers/reviewers for doctrine, hadith attribution, jurisprudence and age suitability.
- A grade-level draft cannot become teaching-ready without actual lessons, sources, teacher keys, accessible checks, review and agreed schedule under the [curriculum model](../../docs/curriculum-model.md).

## Decisions before adopting a course

- Local scholarly/denominational approach and how other documented positions will be represented.
- Language, recitation, accessible pedagogy, approved editions and Qur'an translations.
- Source selection and qualified review of hadith, fiqh, biography and historical interpretations.
- Weekly time, sequence, materials, accommodations and real prerequisite evidence.

None of these is silently decided by adding the subject to the matrix.
