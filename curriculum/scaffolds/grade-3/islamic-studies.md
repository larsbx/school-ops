## Proposed objectives (planning labels only)

`G3-IS-P1`–`G3-IS-P6` are local discussion labels, **not canonical outcome IDs**. The criteria below are proposed assessment-design targets, not administered tasks or observed attainment.

| Label | Observable objective | Candidate evidence and review target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `G3-IS-P1` | Use `sūrah`, `āyah`, translation, hadith/report and commentary accurately; distinguish primary text from an explanation. | Classify at least **4/5** vetted and labeled examples, locating source metadata in two. |
| `G3-IS-P2` | Distinguish direct quotation, translator's words, later historical narration, paraphrase and student inference; state when support is unknown. | Classify **3/4** claims without misattributing an invented example to Qur'an or calling an unaudited hadith authenticated. |
| `G3-IS-P3` | Build a *sourced* relative sequence of selected early Makkah accounts, the Hijrah and Madinah, keeping chronology separate from assumed motives or exact dates. | Arrange **four** vetted event cards in source-supported order, cite two, flag an unsupported detail. |
| `G3-IS-P4` | Name and describe `wuḍūʾ` and `ṣalāh` as worship concepts; identify when an explanation is a source description versus juristic interpretation. | Explain two terms and correctly label one selected source-versus-interpretation example; **no practice demonstration**. |
| `G3-IS-P5` | Connect historical accounts to places and sources without inventing dates, routes or map distances. | Make a two-place, four-event annotated timeline/map from teacher-reviewed evidence. |
| `G3-IS-P6` | Present a respectful, checkable historical or textual explanation with a citation, a reason and a source limitation. | Give one correct claim, a traceable source, a reason and an explicitly labeled uncertainty via an accessible response mode. |

## Proposed entry evidence and prerequisite readiness — entry diagnostic

The Grade 2 prototype's `IS2-P..` labels do **not** establish a learner's prerequisites: they are unregistered and no student evidence is available. Administer a separately authored, source-checked **entry check** before deciding pace. Teacher may read instructions aloud and allow oral, drawing or assistive-communication responses; Arabic fluency and writing speed are not prerequisites.

| Entry skill | Proposed task and threshold | If not met |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **E1: read source labels** | With four accurately labeled, teacher-created cards (source locator, quotation, paraphrase, fictional example), classify **3/4**; a classroom invention must not be identified as scripture. | Teach labels using neutral examples and reassess before episode 1. |
| **E2: sequence events** | Order three *neutral invented* before/after cards correctly and give one supported ordering reason. | Practice physical/card-based ordering; reassess before episodes 4–5. |
| **E3: distinguish observation/inference** | In two nonreligious examples, correctly separate a recorded statement from a conclusion **2/2**. | Model claim versus inference; reassess before episodes 2–3 and the final synthesis. |
| **E4: communicate accessibly** | Explain one familiar neutral example via speech, writing, drawing or AAC; identify language and access supports. | Arrange an equivalent response route; **do not mark disability, English, Arabic or prior worship experience as a content failure**. |

**Progression rule:** check and remediate each unmet skill separately; no overall score substitutes for E1–E4. E1 gates source inquiry, E2 gates chronology, E3 gates source interpretation, E4 supplies a valid response mode in every episode. Prerequisite evidence and accommodations for specific children belong in private systems only. These are **candidate thresholds**, not a finalized readiness instrument.

## Proposed sequence and learning progression (not authored lessons)

Six episodes are *sequencing hypotheses*, **not** six approved lessons or an agreed session count. Each includes all three Trivium arts and a proposed check; choose actual task duration, text, translation and materials after review.

| Order | Inquiry and prerequisite | Grammar | Logic / dialectic | Rhetoric and candidate check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | Finding a text (E1) | `sūrah`, `āyah`, locator, translation | Identify which words are source, translation or teacher summary. | Explain how to retrieve a citation; label a sample source card. |
| **2** | What does a source establish? (1, E3) | Report, commentary, paraphrase, inference | Contrast two differently sourced claims and flag an unsupported conclusion. | Produce a short `source says / I infer / unknown` explanation. |
| **3** | Purification/prayer terminology (1–2) | `wuḍūʾ`, `ṣalāh`, description, juristic view | Separate a reviewed description from an interpretation; recognize that a legal conclusion needs scholarship. | Explain two terms with correctly labeled evidence, without performing rites. |
| **4** | Early Makkah sequence (E2, 1–2) | Makkah; approved event terms | Check source type and relative ordering; do not supply invented dates. | Retell two or three events with source attribution. |
| **5** | Hijrah and Madinah (4) | Hijrah, Madinah, timeline, map legend | Test chronology/place assertions and reject unsupported route details. | Annotate a four-event timeline with two sources and vetted place labels. |
| **6** | Synthesis and revision (1–5, E1–E4 addressed) | Recall terminology and citation form | Diagnose an unsupported claim; revise using evidence. | Present the proposed culminating task, receive targeted feedback, correct attribution first. |

Use an episode-level formative check and an additional targeted remediation session if needed. **No weekly frequency, time budget, calendar or lesson materials have been agreed.**

### The Trivium and meaningful Quadrivium interfaces

- **Grammar:** accurate Arabic/English terms, reading/locating text and reporting what a source actually says.
- **Logic:** compare provenance, distinguish interpretation from observation, evaluate inferences and acknowledge unknown/contested claims.
- **Rhetoric:** express a supported account clearly and respectfully, using speech, writing, drawings or AAC.
- **Arithmetic (optional):** count and compare event intervals **only if** vetted dates/precision permit actual numeric work. Event ordering alone is not arithmetic mastery.
- **Geometry (optional):** use a verified map **scale or measured distance** if the selected activity actually develops magnitude/measurement. Merely showing two locations is not a geometry assessment.
- **Astronomy (optional later):** compare actual lunar observations with calendar conventions, if a separately sourced mathematical inquiry is authored; a Hijrah date does not itself demonstrate astronomy.
- **Music:** excluded, including musical/rhythmic memorization activities. Islamic studies does not become a new Quadrivium art.

## Proposed culminating performance — assessment design, not ready

From a **qualified-reviewer-approved** source packet, four referenced event cards, a verified Makkah–Madinah place map and labeled vocabulary cards, ask the learner to:

1. Put four historical events in the approved order and give source locators for at least two.
2. Identify which of two statements is source text/translation/report/commentary or a learner's inference; label unsupported assertions as unknown.
3. Explain one selected `wuḍūʾ` or `ṣalāh` term without demonstrating or reporting personal observance.
4. Place two vetted locations on the provided map without fabricating a route or distance.
5. Give a clear, cited conclusion and one reason; acknowledge one evidence limit.

Use an oral, written, illustrated or assistive format; source cards remain available. **Candidate scoring rubric:** four dimensions, 0 (unsupported) / 1 (partial) / 2 (accurate and checkable):

| Dimension | Full-credit evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Source custody | Two correct source locators; quotation/translation/paraphrase accurately distinguished. |
| Chronology and place | Four vetted events in order; both locations correct; no unsupported date or route. |
| Conceptual vocabulary | Correct term description; source and juristic interpretation distinguished. |
| Explanation | Supported claim and reason, respectfully expressed, with a labeled uncertainty. |

**Proposed success review:** **6/8** or better **and** no uncorrected false attribution to Qur'an or hadith; then review the criterion's fairness and reassess gaps. This is **not** a ready test, teacher key, standard or report of attainment. A real assessment must supply exact authorized passages/cards, source locators, event order, model responses, permitted prompts, accommodations, administration conditions and scoring exemplars.

## Teacher and source guidance

- **Source selection:** for Qur'an give the sūrah/āyah and identify exact Arabic/translation/tafsīr edition; for hadith give collection and report/edition identifiers and evidence for *any* claimed authenticity grade. Attribute historical claims to their actual source, and mark uncertainty. Do not confuse a later sīrah narrative with a Qur'anic statement.
- **Fiqh and viewpoint:** have qualified scholars review religious descriptions; name a school/scholar when a juristic position is invoked, note documented differences respectfully, and do not treat this investigation as a fatwa.
- **Learner dignity:** **Do not grade private faith**, religious sincerity, compulsory prayer, home routines, recitation or family adherence. Do not assume all learners are Muslim.
- **Access:** inspect language needs, large print and assistive options; allow comparable oral/visual responses. Identify translation/access and age suitability before class use.
- **Feedback:** demonstrate source labeling, use guided comparison, then independent explanation; remediate attribution before fluency or presentation polish. Preserve uncertain claims rather than filling gaps with invented facts.
- **Materials/rights:** keep third-party materials by reference unless permission to copy is verified, and retain a source/review ledger. Store student-specific results and accommodations privately.
- **Cross-disciplinary honesty:** maps, timelines and calendars are possible connections, not proof that arithmetic, geometry or astronomy outcomes were taught.
