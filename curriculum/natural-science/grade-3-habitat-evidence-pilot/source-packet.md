# Fictional habitat evidence packet (original, no external quotations)

**SYNTHETIC CLASSROOM DATA, NOT FIELD OBSERVATIONS.** The following *assumptions and records are invented*; they do not establish facts about actual species, habitat populations, individual animals or ecological causes. Cite labels M, S and T accurately.

## M — given food-web model, not a discovered relationship

- **M1** Within this **made-up** model, a plant is food for a beetle.
- **M2** Within this model, a bird eats beetles.
- **M3** Food-to-eater arrows therefore are **plant → beetle** and **beetle → bird**.
- **M4** A two-arrow **plant → beetle → bird** path does **not** establish a direct **plant → bird** feeding arrow. It is not supplied as a direct relation here.
- **M5** Sunlight is a nonliving energy input, **not** an eater or prey. The model is incomplete and neither copied from a source nor independently field-verified.

## S — invented notebook for one imaginary plot

Each number is a count of **sightings**, NOT distinct living beetles/birds. A creature may be seen again in another round. There is no recorded detectability, observer consistency or population survey.

| Round | Minutes looking | Beetle sightings | Bird sightings |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| A | 5 | 6 | 2 |
| B | 5 | 4 | 2 |
| C | 5 | 5 | 1 |
| D | 10 | 9 | 3 |

- A→B difference is **2 fewer beetle sightings**, not proven loss of two beetles.
- A+B+C is **15 sightings** in three 5-minute rounds; it may include repeat individuals.
- A=**6 sightings / 5 minutes** and D=**9 / 10 minutes**. Looking durations differ, so raw counts alone cannot establish a population increase.
- Either increased hiding/detection differences or a population change could fit the A/B sighting pattern. Neither is proved.

## T — separate invented two-plot, one-day comparison

| Plot | Minutes looking | Beetle sightings |

North has **3 more sightings** for the stipulated five-minute observations. This does not prove North contains more actual beetles, that food caused the difference, or that two plots have matched visibility. Repeat same-duration rounds under documented conditions for a stronger comparison, not a causal guarantee.
