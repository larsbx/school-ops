# Grade 3 habitat evidence — original learner packet (draft)

This public packet is **practice and exposed reference material, not a secure test**. Use the [fictional source packet](source-packet.md), not real-life claims. Provide a reason and cite M/S/T where requested. Untimed; oral, tactile, large-print, dictation and AAC explanations are alternatives. Never write a name or personal information here.

## E1–E4 independent entry checks (neutral prerequisites)

**E1.** A fictional notebook says "**3 dots were marked**." A classmate says "**insects must have marked them**." Which is a recorded statement and which is an unsupported inference? Explain.

**E2.** An observer marks 4 sightings, then 3 sightings. Give the total with the word *sightings*. Does that prove seven **different** creatures appeared? Why?

**E3.** A made-up card says "**bread → bird**" means "a bird eats bread." Which is food and which is eater? Does it mean bread eats a bird?

**E4.** A drawing of a **blue paper boat** is shown. Describe what the drawing supports and one claim about an actual boat it cannot support. Respond in an accessible format.

## G1–G8 guided learner work (supported rehearsal)

**G1 (P1).** M1 is a given feeding rule, S/A has 6 beetle sightings. Label each record's type; which is a real field observation?

**G2 (P2).** Draw M1's food-to-eater arrow and explain its direction.

**G3 (P2).** Add M2's arrow. Does the linked path give a **direct** feeding arrow from plant to bird? Explain with M4.

**G4 (P3).** Compute A minus B beetle sightings, with units. Does it prove the plot lost that number of beetles?

**G5 (P3).** Add A+B+C beetle sightings. Does the total necessarily count distinct insects?

**G6 (P3).** Compare A and D looking durations. Why does D's larger raw sighting count fail to prove more living beetles?

**G7 (P3/P4).** State two **different** causes consistent with A's 6 and B's 4 sightings. Which cause has been proven?

**G8 (P4).** Rewrite "North has more beetles because it has more food" as a supported observation plus a limitation; propose a better repeated comparison.

## R1–R10 independent-looking *public reference* questions

**R1 (P1).** Classify (a) M1 "plant is food for beetle", (b) S/A "6 beetle sightings", (c) "more food must have caused North's higher count". Which statement is actual field data?

**R2 (P2).** Draw **both direct** food-to-eater arrows among the plant, beetle and bird; explain the arrow convention.

**R3 (P1/P2).** Is a direct plant→bird feeding arrow established merely because plant→beetle→bird is a two-arrow path? Cite M4.

**R4 (P3).** Give the change from A to B beetle **sightings** with units. Does that prove a population decrease?

**R5 (P3).** Total A, B and C beetle sightings. Does this prove that many distinct beetles exist?

**R6 (P3).** D lists 9 and A lists 6 beetle sightings. Compare looking durations and decide whether a larger living population has been proved.

**R7 (P1/P3).** Offer two distinct explanations compatible with A=6 and B=4 sightings. What *causal* explanation has the notebook established?

**R8 (P3).** Compute T/North minus T/South sightings with units. Can these single observations isolate the food effect or population sizes?

**R9 (P4).** Design a more comparable repeat between North/South, specifying observation duration, one controlled or recorded condition and a remaining uncertainty.

**R10 (P4).** Write or present a short conclusion from **S rounds A, B and C**, giving at least one numeric fact, the S locator and a reason that a real population trend is **not** established.

**Unscored discussion:** What safety and independently reviewed scientific sources would be necessary for a real-world habitat study? No religious, private-life or fieldwork disclosure is required.
