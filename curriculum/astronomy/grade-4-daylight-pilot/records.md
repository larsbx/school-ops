# Fictional astronomy records — learner packet

All entries are invented for arithmetic and evidence practice. They do not describe any real location, year, forecast or sky observation. A fixed hypothetical clock is used throughout; no clock changes. Sunrise and sunset are labels for recorded events, not instructions to view the Sun. Use only the supplied records.

## Daylight ledger

Site Alder and Site Birch are fictional. A dash means no record, not zero.

| Site | Date label | Sunrise | Sunset |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Alder | Jan 15 | 08:10 | 16:40 |
| Alder | Apr 15 | 06:30 | 18:50 |
| Alder | Jul 15 | 05:20 | 20:40 |
| Alder | Oct 15 | 07:10 | 17:50 |
| Alder | Nov 15 | 07:40 | — |
| Birch | Jul 15 | 06:00 | 18:00 |

All clock readings use 24-hour notation. An interval is the time from the first event to the second on the same date. A comparison of change over dates must use the same site and clock convention; comparing different sites answers a different question.

## Direction strip

Use a simplified horizon-bearing model, with north at 0 degrees, east at 90, south at 180 and west at 270; 360 is the same direction as 0. Bearings increase clockwise as viewed on a map from above. One strip cell is 15 degrees: label positions 0 through 24; position 24 repeats north. The strip encodes direction, not distance or elevation. A clockwise turn crossing north can be counted to 360 and then from 0.
