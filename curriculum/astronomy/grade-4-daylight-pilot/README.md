# Grade 4 astronomy — daylight intervals and sky-direction evidence

**Draft, unregistered, not ready.** Local labels D1–D4 are not stable IDs; canonical IDs remain unassigned. Four proposed 25-minute episodes, independent entry/rechecks and a separate 25-minute reference form; not an adopted timetable. Intended Grade 4, actual starting skills unknown.

This extends the Grade 4 [planning scaffold](../../scaffolds/grade-4/astronomy.md). Grade 2 [sky records](../../mathematics/grade-2/units/sky-records/README.md) and Grade 3 shadow investigation (open PR #51) are not prerequisites presumed taught: here learners compute elapsed daylight across noon, compare dates with a fixed clock convention, encode horizon direction on a scale and bound claims from sampled directions. This is not a shadow-length investigation or a calendar-rule lesson.

| Local outcome | Observable work | Guided / reference evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| D1 | Compute a sunrise-to-sunset interval using hours/minutes; distinguish clock reading from duration. | G1–G2 / S1–S2 |
| D2 | Compare same-site dated intervals on a common scale; identify a missing value and reject a mismatched comparison. | G3–G4 / S3–S5 |
| D3 | Encode horizon bearings on a 15-degree-per-cell strip; compare clockwise changes including wrap at north. | G5–G6 / S6–S7 |
| D4 | Order apparent-direction samples and explain what they support and cannot establish about unsampled times or causes. | G7–G8 / S8–S10 |

Astronomy is the primary Quadrivium art: ordered sky observations and apparent changes. Arithmetic measures intervals; geometry represents direction. Each episode uses naming/notation (grammar), testing evidence (logic), and a checkable explanation (rhetoric). Music is excluded.

## Materials and progression

Teacher prints [fictional records](records.md), [entry learner prompts](entry-learner.md) and [entry keys/rechecks](entry-teacher.md), [teaching sequence](lessons.md), [guided prompts](guided-learner.md) and [guided keys](guided-teacher.md). Use blank number lines, paper, compass-direction labels and optional tactile strips; no outdoor equipment or live observation is required. Demonstrate each prerequisite independently before its dependent lesson; reteach and use a different recheck. Do not substitute grade enrollment for evidence.

The [reference learner form](draft-assessments/reference-learner.md) and [teacher key](draft-assessments/reference-teacher.md) are fixed, publicly exposed examples, not a secure assessment. Read aloud, use tactile tick marks or equivalent labeled text, allow oral or written reasoning. Provide a blank clock/strip or calculator only if approved for a noncalculation target; never supply solved values or infer mastery from practice.

## Source custody, safety and owner decisions

All numbers, sites, dates and directional samples are original synthetic exercise records, not measurements, forecasts or ephemerides. No external readings or images copied. Real astronomical data, location conventions, scientific accuracy and any external resource rights require review before admission. No direct Sun viewing, optical instruments, unsupervised observations or learner locations. Do not infer causes, actual solstices, latitude or complete trajectories from invented records.

Review scope, prior skills, time, safety, accessibility equivalence, scientific and pedagogical accuracy, fresh unseen parallel tasks, rubric and approved resources before teaching.
