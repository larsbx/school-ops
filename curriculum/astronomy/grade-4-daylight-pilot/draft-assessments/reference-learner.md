# Reference form — daylight evidence

Draft, fixed publicly exposed example; not a secure assessment. Suggested 25 minutes, with no speed criterion. Use these new fictional records only; a blank is unknown. Use a blank clock, strip or table, and oral, tactile or written responses. No solved examples or guided keys.

## New fictional packet

All times use one fixed hypothetical clock. No real observations or location.

| Site | Date | Sunrise | Sunset |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cedar | Feb 10 | 07:25 | 17:05 |
| Cedar | May 10 | 05:50 | 19:10 |
| Cedar | Aug 10 | 06:10 | 19:50 |
| Cedar | Nov 10 | — | 16:45 |
| Elm | May 10 | 06:20 | 18:20 |

Use a horizon strip with 15 degrees per cell, north at 0 and 360, east 90, south 180 and west 270; bearings increase clockwise.

The following separate fictional direction samples are from one hypothetical date at Cedar, not necessarily one of the dates above.

| Time | Bearing (degrees) |
| --- | --- |
| 09:00 | 120 |
| 11:00 | 150 |
| 13:00 | — |
| 15:00 | 240 |
| 17:00 | 270 |

**S1.** Calculate Cedar February duration in hours/minutes; show your method.

**S2.** Calculate Cedar May duration in total minutes; explain how a clock reading differs from a duration.

**S3.** Calculate the three complete Cedar durations in minutes. Give their lengths on a common scale of 100 minutes per cell (or a labeled table); mark November unknown and identify the largest of the complete samples.

**S4.** How many minutes longer is August than February at Cedar? State what dates and site you compared.

**S5.** Someone fills the Cedar November blank using Elm May sunrise. Explain why this does not establish November duration.

**S6.** Mark 120 and 240 degrees on the 15-degree strip. Give their positions and clockwise change.

**S7.** A direction marker turns clockwise from 315 to 15 degrees. Give degrees and cells turned, explaining the wrap.

**S8.** List the recorded bearings in time order, leaving the missing time marked unknown.

**S9.** Compare the 09:00→11:00 and 15:00→17:00 bearing changes. Does equality in these two intervals prove the change in every two-hour interval was equal? Explain.

**S10.** Rewrite “The Cedar record proves the exact bearing at 13:00 and the physical cause of its motion.” Use two recorded time/bearing pairs, distinguish observation from inference and give the limits.
