# Solar predictions for two public places — learner sheet

**Review draft.** These are practice examples. They are not a test of mastery.
Use the companion [record sheet](records.md). It gives calculated solar
predictions for public reference places; it contains no learner locations or
learner observations.

**Do not look directly at the Sun. Do not use binoculars, a telescope, a camera
viewfinder or another optical instrument to look at it.** Work indoors with the
supplied tables and paper or screen. This packet asks for no sky watching,
photography, travel or home measurements.

## How to use the records

- Read the **place, date and clock label** before reading a number. The clocks in
  this packet have fixed offsets; they do not add a summer hour. A clock reading
  says when an event is calculated to occur. A duration says how much time passes.
- An **ephemeris** is a table calculated from an astronomical model. A calculated
  prediction is different from a person observing and recording the sky.
- Here, **daylight interval** means sunset time minus sunrise time using the
  printed calculated times. Actual visibility can differ from the calculation.
- A **bearing** names a direction around the horizon. North is 0 degrees, east
  90, south 180 and west 270. Use **true north**, not the north indicated by an
  uncorrected magnetic compass. A bearing does not say how high the Sun is.
- One direction-strip cell is **15 degrees**. Positions 0 and 24 both mean north;
  position 6 is east, 12 is south and 18 is west. The strip goes clockwise when
  viewed like a map from above. It shows directions, not distances.
- To show a calculated bearing on the strip, use the nearest 15-degree tick.
  If exactly halfway, use the higher tick; turn 360 into 0. This is **rounding**,
  so a strip tick is not an exact source bearing.
- **Withheld** means the exercise intentionally leaves a value out. It is not
  zero and does not mean the service failed. Do not invent it or silently fill it
  from a different place, date or clock convention.

You may answer by speaking, writing, dictating or using labeled/tactile markers.
You may give a table instead of drawing bars. Accurate labels and reasoning
matter; neat drawing, speed and colors do not. Ask for a larger table or one row
at a time if that helps.

## Reading sources and measuring intervals

**C1. Prediction or observation?** The source card says a service calculated
solar positions for a supplied place and time. This packet used those positions
and a stated horizon rule to calculate its sunrise and sunset times. Does that
mean a person stood there and observed each event? Name the kind of information
the record actually supplies. Give one way an observation would be different.

**C2. Greenwich in June.** Use Greenwich on 21 June 2026. Find the interval from
the printed sunrise to sunset. Show a split at noon, or another checkable method.
Give your answer in hours and minutes and then in total minutes. State the clock
convention you used.

**C3. Greenwich in December.** Find the interval for Greenwich on 21 December
2026 in hours and minutes and in total minutes. Someone subtracts only the hour
digits of the two clock readings. Explain why the minute parts must also be used.

## Comparing dates carefully

**C4. Sydney on two dates.** Find both printed sunrise-to-sunset intervals for
Sydney. Which of these two dates has the longer interval? By how many minutes?
Name the place and both dates in your comparison.

**C5. A common-scale comparison.** Put the four complete daylight intervals into
a table with columns for place, date and total minutes. If you choose bars,
use the same scale for every bar; otherwise use the labeled table. Compare June
with December at each place. Does this small table prove the exact longest day
of the year at each place? Explain the limit.

**C6. A missing event.** Use the row explicitly marked as a missing-data control
on the record sheet. Using only the values supplied in that control, can you
calculate its daylight interval? Name the missing information. Could a sunset
from the other public place complete it reliably? Explain using place and date
labels. Explain whether the blank was a service failure or a teaching choice.

## Bearings on the direction strip

**C7. Round and place.** Use the source bearings at 09:00 and 13:00 for
Greenwich on 21 June 2026. For each, name the two neighboring 15-degree ticks, choose
the nearest tick, and give its strip position. Explain why your tick is an
approximation even when its label is a whole number.

**C8. A paper marker crosses north.** This is an invented strip exercise, not a
solar prediction. A marker turns **clockwise** from 345 degrees to 30 degrees.
How many degrees and how many cells does it turn? Explain how you crossed north.
Does this instruction prove which way the Sun moved at either public place?

## Reasoning from a few samples

**C9. Keep the gap.** Use the learner bearing series for Greenwich on
21 December 2026. Read its time/bearing pairs in time order. Leave the withheld 11:00 value marked
unknown. Can the exact 11:00 bearing be recovered from this printed series alone?
Explain what additional information would be needed.

**C10. Compare equal time gaps.** Use the complete Greenwich bearing series
for 21 June 2026. Compare 09:00 to 11:00 with 11:00 to 13:00. Give the
clockwise changes between their rounded strip bearings. Are those changes equal? Would two equal rounded changes, if they
occurred, prove equal exact changes at all other times? Explain.

**C11. What is supported?** For each statement, say whether it describes a
calculated source result, a display choice made for this packet, a model
assumption, or an unsupported claim. Give a reason.

1. “The service calculated a bearing for the stated place, date and time.”
2. “We show bearings on ticks spaced 15 degrees apart.”
3. “A learner observed the Sun at every time in the table.”
4. “The two dates in the daylight table prove the exact longest day of the year.”
5. “The sunrise/set model uses a level horizon without hills or buildings
   blocking it.”

**C12. Write a careful report.** Choose two supplied time/bearing pairs from one
place and date. Write, say or dictate a short report that:

- names the place, date and fixed clock;
- gives the two supplied time/bearing pairs and says the strip values are rounded;
- says they are calculated predictions, not direct observations;
- explains one thing the missing-sample table cannot establish; and
- explains why these few samples alone do not establish a physical cause or the
  full path between the times.

Use your own words. A clear spoken explanation is as valid as a written one.
