# Companion teaching sequence — review draft

**Draft, unregistered, not approved for teaching.** This sequence extends the
pilot's local outcomes D1–D4; C1–C12 are local task labels, not registered IDs.
The four proposed episodes below are 25 minutes each. This is a reviewable time
budget, not a class timetable or a claim about any learner's starting skills.

Use the [learner sheet](learner.md), its linked record tables and blank clocks,
number lines, labeled direction strips or equivalent text. Keep the
[teacher key](teacher.md) separate. The original [fictional
packet](../records.md) remains available for controlled teaching and fresh
arithmetic examples. Never mix a fictional value into a sourced table.

## Safety and access

This is an indoor, paper or screen activity. **Do not look directly at the Sun.
Do not use binoculars, a telescope, a camera viewfinder or another optical
instrument to look at it.** No outdoor observation, shadow experiment, learner
location, travel or home data collection is part of this packet.

Offer read-aloud text, enlarged high-contrast tables, one row at a time, a plain
text list of each table row, labeled tactile ticks and oral or dictated
responses. Color, sight, handwriting, precise drawing and speed are not criteria.
A learner may state a strip position instead of placing a marker. An adult may
scribe exactly what the learner says without supplying a calculation or reason.
An approved calculator can support a reasoning-only task; do not treat that
response as evidence of unaided duration arithmetic. Keep access support when
worked examples are removed.

## Prerequisites and feedback

Use the pilot's [entry prompts](../entry-learner.md) and
[keys with separate rechecks](../entry-teacher.md). E1 gates duration arithmetic;
E2 plus duration evidence gates date comparisons; E3 gates scaled strips; E4
plus direction evidence gates sampled-data reasoning. Grade enrollment or earlier
practice is not evidence that these prerequisites are present.

The companion adds new representations. Teach and check them before their
associated tasks; these are not assumed prior knowledge:

| Local check | Prompt after a different worked example | Evidence and feedback |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P1: rounding a direction | These numbers are invented arithmetic examples, not Sun data. Which 15-degree tick is nearest to 112 degrees? Explain using its two neighboring ticks. Then use the stated tie rule for 112.5 degrees. | 105 degrees: distances 7 and 8 degrees. At 112.5 the distances are equal, so use the higher tick, 120 degrees. If needed, model with 81 degrees (75; distances 6 and 9), then recheck 142 degrees (135; distances 7 and 8) and 142.5 degrees (150). Read decimal values aloud; use marked midpoints or a tactile number line. |
| P2: fixed clocks and dates | Invented example: a reference table always uses UTC+02:00. Does it add a summer hour automatically? What other labels, besides 09:00, identify a calculated sample? | No: the declared offset stays fixed. Need date, site and clock convention as well as time. If incomplete, compare two labeled cards; recheck with UTC−03:00 and a different date. No world-time arithmetic is required of the learner. |
| P3: source type | A service calculates where the Sun would appear for a supplied place and time. No observer records the sky. Is the resulting row a prediction or a direct observation? What would a blank row mean? | A calculated prediction; a blank means the requested value is unavailable in this record, not zero. If incomplete, contrast an invented thermometer reading with a model calculation; recheck with a calculated tide table. Explain whether a blank was deliberately withheld or not obtained from the service. |

For each check, accept equivalent oral, written or tactile reasoning. Reteach the
specific missing component and use a different recheck. Do not combine a strong
response in one prerequisite with a missing response in another to declare the
learner ready for its dependent task. Record actual evidence only in the approved
private system.

## Episode 1 — Reading a prediction and measuring an interval (D1; C1–C3)

| Work | Minutes |
| --- | --- |
| Grammar: distinguish a source, a calculation, an observation, sunrise, sunset, clock reading and duration. Read the site, date and fixed-clock labels before any number. | 5 |
| Logic: use a clearly fictional interval from the original packet to model a split at noon. Ask why two event readings can give an interval but one event reading cannot. | 7 |
| Apply: C1–C3 with the sourced reference rows. Allow a blank number line; remove the worked answer while retaining access supports. | 9 |
| Rhetoric: explain one result with site, date, unit and the phrase “from the printed calculated times.” | 4 |

Check: both event readings are used correctly, arithmetic is auditable, and the
result is described as a duration derived from calculated values. If the learner
only subtracts hour digits, return to regrouping minutes before comparisons.
Minute rounding does not make the physical sunrise exact to the minute.

## Episode 2 — Comparing dates without filling gaps (D1–D2; C4–C6)

| Work | Minutes |
| --- | --- |
| Grammar: distinguish site, hemisphere, date, sample, common scale, complete row and withheld value. | 5 |
| Logic: compare two dates at one site before comparing sites. Explain that a missing value is not a short bar or zero daylight. | 7 |
| Apply: C4–C6. Build a common-scale table or bars; label every row. Keep the deliberately withheld control visibly marked. | 9 |
| Rhetoric: give one supported comparison and one statement the small table cannot establish. | 4 |

Check: comparison identifies the same site and the two dates; durations and units
agree; the missing duration stays unknown. The data support these sampled dates,
not the exact longest day of the year, every date in a season or every place in a
hemisphere. If a learner imports a number from another row, return to matching the
site/date/clock labels and recheck with a different omitted field.

## Episode 3 — Showing a calculated bearing on a coarse strip (D3; C7–C8)

| Work | Minutes |
| --- | --- |
| Grammar: name true north, east, south, west, bearing, clockwise, tick, rounded value and repeated north. Distinguish bearing from altitude. | 5 |
| Logic: teach P1 with invented arithmetic values; model how 360 and 0 name the same direction. A clockwise paper-marker instruction specifies a chosen turn. It is not a claim about the Sun's path. | 7 |
| Apply: C7–C8 using the 15-degree strip or equivalent numbered text positions. | 9 |
| Rhetoric: explain why the rounded tick does not give an exact unrounded bearing. | 4 |

Check: the nearest tick and strip position are correct; tie and north wrap rules
are explicit; units are degrees or cells as appropriate. Count a turn in the
specified direction. Do not teach that the Sun's azimuth must increase clockwise
at every site: the shorter turn between the Sydney June 11:00 and 13:00
rounded bearings goes anticlockwise across north. Endpoint comparisons alone do
not establish a continuous path. Reteach representation separately from
apparent-motion claims.

## Episode 4 — Reporting only what the samples support (D4; C9–C12)

| Work | Minutes |
| --- | --- |
| Grammar: distinguish supplied sample, withheld value, inferred value, prediction, observation and explanation of cause. | 5 |
| Logic: compare two equal time gaps. Ask whether equality of rounded changes would establish equality of unrounded changes or continuous motion. It would not. | 7 |
| Apply: C9–C12. Keep gaps marked and use explicit time/bearing pairs in the report. | 9 |
| Rhetoric: read a bounded report to a partner or dictate it; the partner identifies its evidence and its limits. | 4 |

Check: the sample order and labels are correct, the missing value is not invented,
and the explanation separates calculated values from actual observations and
from causal claims. Do not demand a particular grammatical style when the
scientific and mathematical reasoning is clear.

## Review and progression

These exposed tasks support guided practice and author review. They are not a
secure assessment, and completion does not establish mastery. Use the
[teacher criteria](teacher.md) to give component-specific feedback. Before any
teaching or progression decision, the owner reviews scientific/source accuracy,
resources and reuse, age suitability, accessibility equivalence, time budget,
scoring and fresh unseen parallel forms. No registration, readiness promotion,
learner result or adopted schedule is created by this sequence.
