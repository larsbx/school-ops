# Solar companion — teacher key and review criteria

**Teacher only. Draft, unregistered, not approved for teaching.** C1–C12 are
exposed practice tasks, not secure assessment items or evidence of mastery.
Use the [learner sheet](learner.md), [records](records.md),
[source and method notes](sources.md), and [teaching sequence](lessons.md).
The original [synthetic packet](../records.md) remains unchanged.

## Administration, access and safety

The four proposed 25-minute episodes are a reviewable plan, not a schedule.
No speed score. Allow pauses, reading support, enlarged/high-contrast text,
one-row presentation, labeled tactile markers and oral, dictated or written
reasoning. Let the learner state strip positions or provide a labeled table
instead of drawing. A scribe may record the learner's words without repairing
answers. Keep teacher keys out of learner delivery. For a reasoning-only task,
an approved calculator may remove arithmetic load, but then the answer is not
evidence of unaided duration calculation. Keep actual learner records private.

**No direct Sun viewing.** Do not use binoculars, telescopes, camera viewfinders
or other optical instruments to look at the Sun. This packet requires no outdoor
observations, learner coordinates, photographs or travel. Every proposed task can
be completed from the supplied records indoors.

## What the source evidence means

NASA/JPL Horizons supplies **calculated apparent solar positions**, not field
observations. This packet derives sunrise and sunset estimates from those
positions using its stated standard horizon rule; the event times are **authored
derivations**, not a JPL-issued sunrise/sunset table. The Greenwich comparison
against USNO is a separate check described in the source notes. Do not claim that
all event times were independently supplied by two services.

The reference coordinates identify public places. The model's selected zero
ellipsoid height and standard horizon are calculation assumptions, not a survey
of a rooftop, terrain or the visible horizon. Fixed UTC+00:00 at Greenwich and
UTC+10:00 at Sydney are this packet's clocks; they intentionally do not follow
summer clock changes. A service's time scale and a civil clock are different
metadata; preserve the technical distinctions in the source notes.

The learner computes exact arithmetic **on the displayed rounded values**.
That does not make actual sunrise, sunset, a physical daylight interval or an
unrounded bearing exact. A 15-degree tick is within 7.5 degrees of the source
number under the stated circular rounding rule. This is a representation bound,
not a bound on the ephemeris model's physical error. Errors at two rounded
endpoints can affect their difference. Do not infer equal or unequal continuous
rates merely from coarse ticks.

## Complete answer key

### C1 — Prediction or observation? (D4; source vocabulary)

No person is documented as observing these events. The provider calculated solar
positions for a specified place and time; the packet used a stated horizon rule
to derive its event-time estimates. These are predictions from a model. An
observation would require an actual event or measurement recorded at the place
and time, with its method and conditions. Accept a comparable distinction without
requiring the word “ephemeris.”

### C2 — Greenwich in June (D1)

Greenwich, 21 June 2026, fixed UTC+00:00: **03:43 to 20:21**.

- 03:43 to noon is 8 h 17 min.
- Noon to 20:21 is 8 h 21 min.
- Total **16 h 38 min = 998 min**.

Accept subtraction of total minutes or another valid method. Require both event
readings, unit, site/date and the fixed clock convention. This is the interval
from the printed calculated times.

### C3 — Greenwich in December (D1)

Greenwich, 21 December 2026, fixed UTC+00:00: **08:03 to 15:53**.

- 08:03 to noon is 3 h 57 min.
- Noon to 15:53 is 3 h 53 min.
- Total **7 h 50 min = 470 min**.

Subtracting only 15 minus 8 gives 7 h and omits the 50-minute difference between
the minute parts. Accept an equivalent auditable method and explanation.

### C4 — Sydney on two dates (D1–D2)

Both rows use the Sydney public reference point and fixed **UTC+10:00**.

| Date | Printed interval | Duration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 21 June 2026 | 07:00 to 16:54 | **9 h 54 min = 594 min** |
| 21 December 2026 | 04:41 to 19:05 | **14 h 24 min = 864 min** |

December has the longer displayed interval: **864 − 594 = 270 min = 4 h 30 min**.
Require the same site, the two dates and their common fixed clock. These two
samples do not establish every day's duration.

### C5 — A common-scale comparison (D2)

| Public reference place | Date | Interval from displayed minutes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Greenwich | 21 June 2026 | **998 min** |
| Greenwich | 21 December 2026 | **470 min** |
| Sydney | 21 June 2026 | **594 min** |
| Sydney | 21 December 2026 | **864 min** |

For Greenwich, June exceeds December by **528 min = 8 h 48 min**. For Sydney,
December exceeds June by **270 min = 4 h 30 min**. The two public places show
opposite June-versus-December comparisons in these records. Accept a correctly
labeled numeric table or common-scale bars without demanding fine drawing.

The four rows do **not** establish the exact longest date of the year: other
dates have not been compared. Nor do they establish every place's seasonal
pattern. Do not change “longer of these two samples” to “the longest day.”

### C6 — A missing event (D2; evidence limit)

The missing-data control M1 is a deliberately restricted copy of the Greenwich
21 December 2026 row. It supplies sunrise **08:03** but withholds sunset. Using
only M1, its interval is **unknown**, not zero. A sunset from Sydney is for a
different reference point and cannot supply the missing Greenwich event. A row
from a different date is also not a substitute for the requested date.

The omission is an **authored teaching choice**, not a source failure or proof
that the Sun did not set. The complete original row is preserved for provenance
and elsewhere in the record sheet. If a learner says “the full table gives 15:53,
so the original row gives 470 min, but M1 alone cannot,” accept this as correct
reasoning: the restriction is on the evidence used, not a claim that the value is
unknowable. Do not punish a learner for noticing the deliberate duplicate.

### C7 — Round and place (D3)

Greenwich, 21 June 2026, fixed UTC+00:00:

| Time | Source bearing | Neighboring ticks | Nearest tick | Strip position |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 09:00 | 111.371720 degrees | 105 and 120 | **105 degrees** | **7** |
| 13:00 | 207.264056 degrees | 195 and 210 | **210 degrees** | **14** |

For 09:00 the distances to the ticks are 6.371720 and 8.628280 degrees; for 13:00
they are 12.264056 and 2.735944 degrees. The learner need not perform six-place
decimal subtraction: correctly identifying the nearer tick on a labeled line and
explaining the comparison is equivalent. Read the decimals aloud or mark the
neighboring ticks and midpoint. Do not round first to a whole degree and then to
a 15-degree tick.

The strip values are approximations to the source numbers. A whole-number label
is a choice of display scale, not evidence that the actual bearing has no
fractional part. The source's printed decimal places are also not a claim of
physical accuracy to six places.

### C8 — A paper marker crosses north (D3)

From 345 degrees clockwise to north is **15 degrees**, then north to 30 degrees
is **30 degrees**. Total **45 degrees = 3 cells**, clockwise. North can be written
as 360 or 0; strip positions 23 to 2 pass the repeated north position.

This is an explicitly invented paper-marker turn. It says nothing about which
way the Sun moved at either place. For the June Sydney ticks, the shorter turn
from 15 degrees at 11:00 to 345 degrees at 13:00 is 30 degrees anticlockwise
across north. The corresponding clockwise marker turn would be 330 degrees.
Neither endpoint calculation alone establishes the continuous physical path.
Never treat a large positive wrap in the printed labels as proof of a large
clockwise sweep by the Sun.

### C9 — Keep the gap (D4)

Greenwich, 21 December 2026, fixed UTC+00:00:

| Time | Learner's supplied rounded bearing |
| --- | --- |
| 09:00 | **135 degrees** |
| 11:00 | **Withheld / unknown from this restricted series** |
| 13:00 | **195 degrees** |
| 15:00 | **225 degrees** |

The exact 11:00 bearing cannot be recovered from these supplied samples alone.
A fresh calculation from an adequately specified ephemeris model, or retrieval of
the preserved source row, can supply a calculated value. An actual measurement
would be a different kind of evidence. Simply placing the answer halfway between
09:00 and 13:00 assumes a rule not given by the samples.

Teacher provenance only: the preserved JPL row is **166.293650 degrees**, rounded
to **165 degrees**, strip position **11**. This is deliberately absent from the
learner series. Even though the midpoint of the neighboring rounded ticks happens
to be 165, that coincidence does not establish the missing unrounded value. Do
not use the concealed teacher answer as evidence that interpolation was justified.

### C10 — Compare equal time gaps (D4)

Greenwich, 21 June 2026, fixed UTC+00:00:

- 09:00 to 11:00: **150 − 105 = 45 degrees clockwise = 3 cells** in two hours.
- 11:00 to 13:00: **210 − 150 = 60 degrees clockwise = 4 cells** in two hours.

The displayed rounded changes are **not equal**. These are differences between
endpoints, not a measured continuous trace. If two rounded changes had been
equal, that would not prove equal exact changes: rounding can conceal
small differences. It would also not establish other time intervals or a
complete path. The full source numbers permit separate numerical checks, but
learners are answering about the stated coarse display.

### C11 — What is supported? (D4)

| Statement | Classification and reason |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Service calculated a bearing for the stated place/date/time | **Calculated source result**: the stored ephemeris row supplies that value with its request metadata. It is not an observation. |
| 2. We show bearings on 15-degree ticks | **Packet representation choice**: it keeps the pilot's direction scale; the provider numbers have finer printed resolution. |
| 3. A learner observed every table time | **Unsupported**: no learner observation, location or record was collected. |
| 4. Two dates prove the exact longest day | **Unsupported**: a comparison of two samples cannot exclude every unsampled date. |
| 5. The sunrise/set model uses a level horizon without hills or buildings blocking it | **Model assumption**: this defines the idealized calculation; it is not a survey showing that the public site has no obstructions. |

Accept accurate paraphrases. Do not accept “source verified” as meaning the
physical sky was independently observed or that all model assumptions were tested.

### C12 — A careful report (D4; rhetoric)

One complete model response:

> At the Greenwich public reference point on 21 June 2026, using fixed
> UTC+00:00, the calculated bearings at 09:00 and 13:00 are shown as 105 and
> 210 degrees on our rounded strip. These values come from an ephemeris
> calculation, not someone watching the Sun. The December learner table leaves
> 11:00 out, so those supplied samples alone cannot establish its exact bearing.
> These few samples alone do not show the whole path between the times or prove
> the physical cause of the apparent change.

Accept any two correct supplied time/bearing pairs from one place/date with its
clock label, explicit rounding and prediction/observation distinction, and both
limits: missing exact value plus continuous-path/causal evidence. The report may
refer to the separate December missing-sample table as above; it must not imply
that the June table has a missing 11:00 row. Alternative prose, oral response or
accurate dictation can satisfy the same criteria.

## Outcome evidence and proposed feedback rubric

| Outcome | Tasks | Evidence to retain in private review |
| --- | --- | --- |
| D1: duration arithmetic | C2–C4 | Uses the relevant event readings and fixed clock, handles minutes, labels duration, explains a valid method. |
| D2: bounded seasonal comparison | C4–C6 | Compares dates at the same site, uses common units/scale, labels the selected samples, preserves the deliberate missing value and rejects invalid substitution. |
| D3: horizon-bearing representation | C7–C8 | Uses true-north clockwise scale, chooses the nearest tick under the stated rule, gives strip positions and a correct requested wrap turn, distinguishes representation from solar motion. |
| D4: source and sampled-data reasoning | C1, C9–C12 | Separates predictions, authored derivations and observations; orders samples; preserves gaps; limits conclusions about exact values, other times and causes. |

Proposed task rubric: **complete** means all named components are correct and the
requested reason is supplied; **partial** means a meaningful component is correct
but another is missing; **revisit** means no relevant correct component or an
unsupported value/observation is asserted as fact. Annotate the missing component
rather than assigning an aggregate pass. Do not let arithmetic correctness cancel
an invented missing datum or an observation claim.

For a progression decision, the owner must approve a fresh parallel form and
outcome-specific criteria. These fixed exposed tasks and their public keys cannot
establish independent mastery. Scientific/source review, age and accessibility
review, resource rights, teaching time, scoring and course registration remain
owner decisions. Neither automated tests nor a successful code review supply
teaching approval.


## Teacher-controlled parallel-form plan — proposal for owner review

This is a concrete plan for a later independent check, **not an issued form or a
claim of fresh or secure assessment**. The present C1–C12 prompts, keys and source
records are exposed. Simply changing names, reordering these rows or hiding a
worked answer does not establish independence. Ephemerides are publicly
reproducible; a new question sheet still needs controlled administration and
private learner evidence.

1. **Select new evidence before administration.** Keep the two public reference
   points and fixed clock conventions. Retrieve and verify two additional dated
   ephemerides per site, for example 20 June and 20 December 2026, following the
   same extraction checks. These dates are proposals, not values already
   obtained or admitted by this packet. Record the exact requests, response
   digests, horizon assumptions, event rounding and any missing-source status.
   Do not invent replacement times to make the arithmetic convenient.
2. **Construct one parallel form against the same outcomes.** Include a complete
   displayed-time interval for D1; two same-site dated intervals and an expressly
   withheld event for D2; two new source bearings plus one explicitly synthetic
   north-wrap marker turn for D3; and a short ordered series with one deliberately
   withheld sample and an evidence-limited report for D4. Keep the distinction
   between actual provider gaps and authored omissions. Match the arithmetic
   demand and reasoning criteria to this packet without copying its numerical
   answers. Do not add new outcome requirements through the form.
3. **Check the form and key separately.** Regenerate the numerical answers from
   the preserved inputs and have a reviewer check each prompt against its key,
   including omission controls, fixed dates/clocks, one-step 15-degree rounding,
   north wrap and the difference between endpoint changes and a continuous path.
   Human review must check the explanatory criteria; a numeric test cannot judge
   every valid oral explanation. Keep the complete teacher key separate from the
   learner sheet and inspect the latter for answer-bearing links or worked cues.
4. **Approve equivalent access and administration.** Prepare the same content as
   a plain-text sequence of labeled rows, read-aloud text or tactile positions.
   Preserve the mathematical and evidence-limit criteria while allowing the
   response modes described above. Record any calculator permission by target.
   Give no solved examples during the independent check, use no speed criterion,
   and record whether the learner has already encountered that exact form.
5. **Review outcomes individually.** Retain the duration method, valid comparison,
   rounded-position/wrap reasoning and bounded-evidence explanation as separate
   evidence. An invented missing value or unsupported observation claim requires
   feedback even when arithmetic is correct. Reteach the affected component and
   use a separately reviewed variant; do not repeatedly administer an exposed
   form as evidence of independent learning.

The curriculum owner decides whether the sources, tasks, accessibility options,
scoring and administration support the intended interpretation. Record approval
of the exact proposed form before use; automated generation, a clean test run
and this plan do not authorize teaching or establish mastery.
