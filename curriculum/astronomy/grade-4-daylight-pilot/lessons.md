# Four draft Trivium episodes

Use the [record packet](records.md) and [guided prompts](guided-learner.md); teacher keeps the [guided keys](guided-teacher.md) separate. Each proposed episode is 25 minutes, not a scheduled lesson. No direct observation or Sun viewing.

## Episode 1 — Clock reading versus duration (D1)

| Work | Minutes |
| --- | --- |
| Grammar: name sunrise, sunset, same date, clock reading, interval and minute; read 08:10 and 16:40 aloud. | 5 |
| Logic: model a split at noon (08:10 to 12:00 = 3 h 50 min; noon to 16:40 = 4 h 40 min); regroup 90 minutes and test the mistaken subtraction 16 − 8 = 8. | 8 |
| Guided G1–G2: use a blank line, then fade the worked example; ask partner to verify the units. | 8 |
| Rhetoric and independent X1: explain elapsed time to a partner using a different interval, 07:45 to 17:15. | 4 |

X1 key: 9 h 30 min = 570 min (4 h 15 + 5 h 15). Require duration with unit and an auditable split or equivalent arithmetic; reteach regrouping on an unrelated example if missing.

## Episode 2 — Comparing seasonal records (D2)

| Work | Minutes |
| --- | --- |
| Grammar: label date, site, minute, equal scale and unknown; distinguish a sampled date from a whole season. | 5 |
| Logic: compare Alder January and April on one common axis; show how changing site or using a blank as zero can change an apparent trend. | 8 |
| Guided G3–G4: calculate changes and build a four-date table or bar representation, with no fabricated November bar. | 8 |
| Rhetoric and independent X2: tell a peer whether the Nov entry proves less daylight than Oct and why. | 4 |

X2 key: no; November lacks sunset, so interval and comparison are unknown. Require the missing field and the bounded conclusion. For a fresh check use an invented row with sunrise missing.

## Episode 3 — Direction as a scale (D3)

| Work | Minutes |
| --- | --- |
| Grammar: distinguish horizon bearing, clockwise, north, east, south, west, and degrees; label the repeating north ends of a 24-cell strip. | 5 |
| Logic: place 75 and 120 degrees; calculate the 45-degree change by three cells. Contrast position with a turn and demonstrate wrap across north. | 8 |
| Guided G5–G6: mark sample bearings and compute clockwise changes, with strip ticks or labeled tactile cells. | 8 |
| Rhetoric and independent X3: explain the clockwise turn from 345 to 15 degrees, including scale. | 4 |

X3 key: 15 + 15 = 30 degrees, two cells; 345 is position 23, 15 is position 1 after north. Require direction, amount and method. Never treat bearing as linear distance.

## Episode 4 — Apparent motion and evidence limits (D4)

| Work | Minutes |
| --- | --- |
| Grammar: distinguish observed sample, ordered time, missing sample, inference and cause. | 5 |
| Logic: compare 08:00→10:00 and 10:00→12:00 changes (30 and 45 degrees); challenge the claim of equal change in every equal time interval. | 8 |
| Guided G7–G8: build a sample sequence with a blank; argue which claims are warranted. | 8 |
| Rhetoric and independent X4: revise “At 14:00 it was exactly 220 degrees because it was moving clockwise.” | 4 |

X4 key: 14:00 is unknown; the recorded bearings at 12:00 and 16:00 are 180 and 255 degrees but do not supply the exact intervening bearing or a complete path. Require a recorded detail and explicit limit. Review D1–D4 with a fresh unseen form before any progression decision; exposed examples are rehearsal only.
