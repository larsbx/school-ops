# Lesson 1 — measuring the same shadow fairly (DRAFT; not ready)

**Local target:** G3-AST-P1, P3. **Entry:** E3 and E4; remediate before proceeding. **Materials proposed:** paper drawing of vertical stake, ruler, line segments, optional tactile strip. No sunlight or field visit required. **25-minute illustrative episode**, not an adopted timetable. Teacher answers: [worked/exit key](../guided-teacher.md).

| Trivium phase | Minutes |
| --- | ---: |
| Grammar: distinguish object height, shadow length, end point, centimetres | 5 |
| Logic: compare two drawings with different object heights | 7 |
| Guided model: select consistent measurement conditions, G1/G2 | 6 |
| Rhetoric: tell partner a reproducible protocol | 4 |
| Exit X1: defend one missing control | 3 |

**Teacher model, original fiction:** A 20 cm straight stake is shown upright on the same flat surface. Draw a ground line from the point where the stake touches the surface to the **end of its shadow**. The line might be 32 cm at one labelled time. “32 cm” is a **length reading**, not an angle or a claim about the real Sun. Compare a second drawing where the stake's height changed to 40 cm. A shadow-length difference now has at least one confounder, even if the clock labels match.

**Learner:** Complete [G1 and G2](../guided-learner.md). Teacher asks whether a missing ground endpoint, shifted stick or different height permits a fair comparison. Learner identifies at least two fixed conditions and names centimetres.

**X1 — independent response:** A report says, “At 10:00, the same labelled 20 cm stake cast 18 cm of shadow; at 11:00, it cast 12 cm.” An assistant secretly moved the stake between readings. State (a) the recorded numerical difference, (b) one reason the comparison is not controlled, (c) one fix. Accept accessible speech/diagram/typed response with all three elements; see separate key.

**If incomplete:** revisit zero point and length versus object height; compare a neutral 10 cm / 16 cm drawing before repeating with fresh readings. Do not claim a numerical match establishes a physical hypothesis.
