# Lesson 4 — scientific claims and comparison limits (DRAFT; not ready)

**Local targets:** G3-AST-P3, P4. **Entry:** E3/E4; no assumption of actual observational experience. **Materials:** teacher-created claim cards, records, chart, optional tactile grid/AAC. **25-minute authored example**, not an agreed timetable. [Separate key](../guided-teacher.md).

| Trivium phase | Minutes |
| --- | ---: |
| Grammar: observation, confounder, hypothesis, sample and missing entry | 5 |
| Logic: scrutinize height change and missing day-B record | 7 |
| Guided: G6/G8; counterexample to universal inference | 6 |
| Rhetoric: defend claim, limitation and next check | 4 |
| Exit X4: bounded claim plus necessary additional evidence | 3 |

**Author-created claim clinic:** A fictional pair of tables describes two daytime samples from one setup; another compares a 20 cm stake with a 40 cm stake. Use the former to make a **bounded** comparison at matching clock labels and the latter to identify a confounder. Neither claims real data or an astronomical cause.

**Learner:** complete [G6/G8](../guided-learner.md). Model the four-part rhetoric frame: “The *recorded* value __ was __; the table shows __; it does **not** show __; I would need __.” The teacher does not correct an appropriately qualified uncertainty into a stronger claim.

**X4 — independent response:** At 09:00, day A reads 32 cm and day B 30 cm; day B has **no 12:00 record**. State a justified day A versus B comparison, reject or narrow “day B always has shorter shadows,” and identify one further measurement or control. Evidence requires value/units, bounded wording, and a reason for refusing the universal claim.
