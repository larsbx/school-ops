**Fictional constructed log (one unchanged 20 cm stake and origin, same example day):**

| Local clock label | Shadow length |
| --- | --- |
| 09:00 | 32 cm |
| 12:00 | 12 cm |
| 15:00 | 27 cm |

**Teacher models:** subtract 32−12 = 20 cm and 27−12 = 15 cm; 12:00 has the shortest **of these three recorded readings**. Times are three hours apart. A record at 11:00 or 13:00 could change which *instant* is shortest. A clock label called “noon” is **not** verified solar noon; no physical solar claim follows from synthetic numbers.

**Learner:** attempt [G3 and G7](../guided-learner.md) and critique “The Sun is directly overhead at 12:00 everywhere.” Discuss absence of evidence and geographic/seasonal assumptions, without asserting any measured local position.

**X2 — independent response:** Using the fictional table, name the lowest sampled reading with its unit; find the difference from 15:00; finish “among these three readings ...” and say why “shortest over the whole day” is not established. Teacher checks arithmetic, quantifier and missing-data reason independently.
