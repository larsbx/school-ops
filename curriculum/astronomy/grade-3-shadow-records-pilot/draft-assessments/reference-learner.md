# Grade 3 astronomy — fixed reference tasks S1–S10 (learner copy; DRAFT)

**Fixed, publicly exposed** rehearsal examples, **not** a valid unseen independent assessment or teaching-ready item bank. Entire fictional record below is **invented for reasoning practice**; it does not report real sky observation. No outdoor work or personal location record is required. All shadows refer to a hypothetical **unchanged, upright 20 cm stake at one site**, with cm lengths measured on the same hypothetical level ground. On day B, one reading is deliberately absent.

| Fictional day | 08:30 | 11:30 | 14:30 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | 34 cm | 14 cm | 28 cm |
| B | 33 cm | **not recorded** | 26 cm |
| C | 36 cm | 15 cm | 30 cm |

Use explanations as well as numbers. Speech, drawing, enlarged graph, tactile diagram, AAC or a scribe can show the same reasoning. No timing penalty. Do not seek or directly view the Sun.

**S1.** What do the three column headings mean? What is the unit of the numerical entries? State one detail that makes two measurements comparable.

**S2.** Find the time elapsed from 08:30 to 11:30 and from 11:30 to 14:30 on a given day.

**S3.** For day A, identify the largest and the smallest **recorded** length with time labels. State the difference between them.

**S4.** Calculate the difference between day A at 08:30 and day A at 11:30. Show the subtraction and unit.

**S5.** Compare day A and day B at the **same 08:30** time label. Which is shorter and by how much? Can this comparison alone establish that B is shorter at every time? Explain.

**S6.** A friend enters “0 cm” into day B at 11:30. What is wrong? How should it appear on a graph? Would connecting known B points create an observed value at 11:30?

**S7.** Evaluate: “At 11:30 both A and C had the smallest among their *three* recorded lengths.” Is this supported? What stronger all-day conclusion is **not** supported?

**S8.** Graph day A with time on the horizontal axis (08:30, 11:30, 14:30 at equal **3-hour** spacing) and length on the vertical axis (0, 10, 20, 30, 40 cm at equal spacing). Label units and report the three plotted coordinates.

**S9.** A graph designer puts 0, 10, 30, 40 cm at four **equally spaced** vertical ticks but labels them “every 10 cm.” Diagnose the error. State a corrected tick sequence that fits every number in day A.

**S10.** A speaker says: “This proves where the real Sun stood on those dates and proves the exact daily minimum.” Identify **two** independent reasons that conclusion is unsupported, and name one kind of source or additional record needed for a responsible astronomical conclusion.

**Access/administration:** This is a public fixed form for manuscript and feedback review, not a scored claim of learner mastery. Do not store student responses or information about home observations in the repository.
