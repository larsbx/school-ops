# Four original Trivium episodes — DRAFT

**No formal lesson IDs, approved timetable, classroom-ready materials or claimed mastery.** Episodes below are four *illustrative* 25-minute blocks. Read [fictional sources A/B](source-packet.md); use print, tactile event cards, read-aloud, whiteboard, large print and dictated answers as needed. Every lesson uses substantive **Grammar**, **Logic** and **Rhetoric**. Teacher models for X1–X4 are in the [separate key](teacher-guide.md). Entry checks and remediation are [here](entry-teacher.md).

## Episode 1 — What does a word mean here? (G3-LANG-P1; E1/E2)

| Move | Minutes |
| --- | --- |
| Grammar: locate *unfolded* in A2; mark *un-* and *fold*, show a folded paper | 5 |
| Model: use "Please recheck the drawing after the label changes"; *re-* + *check* means check again | 6 |
| Logic: select "opened out" versus "folded again" for A2; demand A2 as reason. In invented "a careless copy", *care* + *-less* gives a clue, but A does not call Noor careless | 9 |
| Rhetoric + X1: teach the word meaning to an absent listener; name one thing A2 cannot prove | 5 |

**X1:** Explain "unfolded" using A2, and state whether A2 proves the route was verified. If a prefix guess disagrees with context, model a physical paper and re-prompt. The teacher distinguishes morphology from an invented claim about a character.

## Episode 2 — Reasoned event order (G3-LANG-P2; E2)

| Move | Minutes |
| --- | --- |
| Grammar: identify A1/A2/A5/A6 as events; label the imperative "Check" a request | 5 |
| Logic: shuffle strips A5, A1, A6, A2; ask why A5 cannot prove a route was walked; use A6 as counterevidence | 7 |
| Coached composition: write/dictate four sentences introducing the torn map, tracing, edit and missing verification, with A labels | 8 |
| Rhetoric + X2: tell a partner the order and whether the text establishes verified route safety | 5 |

**X2:** Put A1/A2/A5/A6 in time order and explain the limits of the phrase "that day." If the learner claims "never", revisit the temporal qualifier. Do not equate a reminder with an inspection.

## Episode 3 — Warrant and numerical countermodel (G3-LANG-P3; E3 plus quantity check)

| Move | Minutes |
| --- | --- |
| Grammar: separate A3 opinion, A4 counterdetail and A6 limit; locate B1/B2/B4 evidence | 4 |
| Logic: refute "the old map is useless" using A4, then test "the revised copy is definitely safe" using A5/A6 | 7 |
| Actual arithmetic: compute B1 **9 − 12 = −3**, B2 **11 − 10 = +1**; model Monday remove 3/add 0 versus remove 5/add 2, both net −3. B4 records neither flow | 9 |
| Rhetoric + X3: give one B-supported claim and one B-unsupported claim with citations | 5 |

**X3:** Explain why B1 does not justify "exactly three people borrowed books." If necessary use 12 counters and two different movement demonstrations. Changes of totals are not counts of persons.

## Episode 4 — Audience, quotation and bounded revision (G3-LANG-P4; E3/E4)

| Move | Minutes |
| --- | --- |
| Grammar: compare exact A5 quotation "Check this route with a teacher." to paraphrase "Noor asked for a check" with A5 attribution | 5 |
| Logic: change "Noor proved the map safe" to a statement no stronger than A5/A6; distinguish action from proposal | 7 |
| Rhetoric: write/say three sentences for a classmate not shown Text A: question, detail with line ID, limit with second ID. Ask partner "How do you know?" then revise | 8 |
| Exit X4: rewrite "They tested and approved the route" with a specific line and a time limit | 5 |

**X4:** Attribute a faithful counterstatement to A5/A6. Feedback: a quote must be exact and marked; a paraphrase can use new words but must preserve the claim. Oral and scribed replies meet the same evidence criterion.
