# Entry checks — teacher key, remediation and rechecks

**Draft / not ready.** Read all prompts, line labels and options aloud on request, enlarge or translate access instructions without altering the fictional evidence, and accept oral, written or pointed explanations. Record evidence only in a private system. Teacher help during an entry attempt makes it *supported practice*, not independent evidence. Use distinct rechecks below; a pass is one complete, independently supported distinction per entry skill, **not** a claim of grade-wide mastery.

| Skill | Full initial key and evidence | If missing: targeted instruction | Independent fresh recheck with answer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| E1 | “Studio opens at nine” is the notice's content; “probably busy” is a prediction unsupported by that notice. Both identified. | Place two strips in columns **said** and **guessed**. Model one neutral example; fade answer cues. | “The ferry sign says boarding starts at three. Priya expects a long queue.” Key: three is reported; long queue is a prediction. |
| E2 | Figurative; rain sounds are compared to drumming. No real drummer follows from the wording. | Contrast “A drummer struck a drum” with “The leaves danced”; ask what physically happened. | “The afternoon raced away.” Key: time felt fast; no literal running. |
| E3 | No. “Five maps were returned to the desk” states quantity/place/day, not a returner's identity; Omar is unsupported. | Underline subject, action, time, actor; identify missing actor. | “On Friday, two boxes reached the lab.” A claim “Asha carried them” is unsupported; the record gives no carrier. |
| E4 | Log first: closed at 11:30; then open at noon. The change happened by noon, but **who, why and exactly when** are unknown. Two ordered facts plus one limit are required. | Use time cards 11:30 and noon; model “before/after,” then hide example. | “At 14:00 the gate was locked”; “at 14:20 it was open.” Key: locked → open; who/why/exact moment unknown. |

**Decision:** E1 and E3 support source comparison; E2 supports Episode 1; E4 supports Episode 4. Where one is not shown, explicitly teach it and repeat with an unseen equivalent prompt; do not report a pass on this example. Language or reading-access needs are not treated as deficits in reasoning.
