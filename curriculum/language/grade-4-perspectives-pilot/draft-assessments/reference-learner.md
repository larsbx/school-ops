# Public reference form — perspectives in a garden

**Draft fixed exemplars / publicly exposed / not a secure or independently unseen assessment.** Untimed; request reading aloud of every line, large print, speech, scribing or line-label pointing. Use *only* the invented cards below; they are not historical records. Teacher solutions are maintained separately.

## Fictional source packet

**Card D — Ada's note, written 16:00**

- **D1.** At 15:05 I saw two yellow flags beside the east path.
- **D2.** The wind whispered through the flags.
- **D3.** I left before the garden inspection was finished.

**Card E — Basil's note, written 16:10**

- **E1.** At 15:25 I entered by the west gate and saw only one yellow flag near the gate.
- **E2.** I wrote, “Someone must have removed a flag,” though I had not looked at the east path.
- **E3.** Later I visited the east path and saw two yellow flags there.

**Card F — partial garden checklist, author omitted**

- **F1.** At 15:10 the checklist records “two flags intact beside east path.”
- **F2.** At 15:28 the checklist records “one flag beside west gate.”
- **F3.** The checklist does not identify who set the flags or whether all positions were checked.

## Ten independent *reference* tasks

**S1.** Identify the figurative phrase in D2 and paraphrase it without inventing a speaking wind.

**S2.** Does D2 prove that a person whispered? Explain one literal claim it *cannot* support.

**S3.** Name the location and time of Ada's direct observation; identify the line.

**S4.** Is Basil's E2 statement observed fact or inference? Quote/point to the word that makes the uncertainty visible.

**S5.** Give the different vantage points in D1 and E1. Do they necessarily contradict each other?

**S6.** Produce a two-step, attributed comparison of D1 and F1. State whether their timings conflict.

**S7.** Does F2 establish that a flag was stolen from the east path? Give one missing piece of evidence.

**S8.** Use E3 and F1 to support a cautious claim about the east-path flags. Give one limitation of the evidence.

**S9.** Revise “Basil proved all the garden flags vanished” into three sentences for someone who has never read the cards, with a line citation and a knowledge limit.

**S10.** A reader asks: “Perhaps the checklist was wrong.” Reply respectfully using at least one witness line and one checklist line; avoid claiming certainty about any person's intentions.

**Response modes:** writing, oral explanations, diagrams with labeled line citations, pointing/selection plus dictated explanation. The criterion is exact source-grounded reasoning, not handwriting speed or confession of private experience.
