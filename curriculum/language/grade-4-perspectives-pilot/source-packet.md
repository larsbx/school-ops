# Original fictional source packet — the blue folders

**Draft classroom fiction, not real eyewitness evidence.** The invented people, times and locations serve a language investigation. The line labels are local references, not external citations. Present A–C without a teacher explanation on learner copies.

## Card A — Leena's account, written at 09:00

- **A1.** At 08:10 I entered the reading room through the west door.
- **A2.** I saw three blue folders on the front table.
- **A3.** The hallway swallowed my footsteps as I turned the corner.
- **A4.** Beside the table, a sign read, “Please leave folders here.”
- **A5.** At 08:15 I left. I did not see anyone move the folders.

## Card B — Noah's account, written at 09:10

- **B1.** I arrived at 08:20, after Leena says she left.
- **B2.** From the doorway, the front table looked bare.
- **B3.** I said, “Someone cleared everything,” but I had not checked the shelves.
- **B4.** When I went inside, I saw three blue folders on the east shelf.
- **B5.** I cannot say who moved them.

## Card C — partial room log, author not recorded

- **C1.** Entry at 08:00: “Three blue folders delivered to the reading room.”
- **C2.** Entry at 08:17: “Blue folders placed on the east shelf for sorting.”
- **C3.** This log gives no name of the person who placed the folders.
- **C4.** The page has no final check of what remained in the room.

## Reading conditions

“A hallway swallowed my footsteps” is **figurative**: it does not report an actual swallowing. A sign, a witness and a log have different vantage points. A log line can record a reported placement without proving who acted, why, or whether it was verified by another person. The texts may support a provisional sequence but do **not** identify the mover or warrant a moral accusation. Treat the fictional log as partially documented, not as infallible proof.
