# Grade 2 teacher/learner source cards and model evidence

**Original teaching adaptation, draft. Do not reproduce as Qur'an or hadith.** These cards provide enough text to run the drafted classification and interpretation activities after qualified review. Use read-aloud, large-print, oral, pointing or drawing responses as appropriate.

## Card Q — teacher summary, *not a Qur'anic translation*

**Source label:** Qur'an, Sūrat al-Ḥujurāt 49:13. **Locator:** 49:13. [External text](https://quran.com/49/13).

**Teacher-created summary:** The verse addresses humanity, mentions different peoples and groups getting to know one another, and describes righteousness as significant before Allah. This is a *summary*; it is not the exact Arabic Qur'anic wording, an approved translation or a complete tafsīr. For direct teaching, an approved teacher must consult the cited verse and agreed translation.

**Question to investigate:** What can we say is *in this summary*? What would require looking at an approved translation or tafsīr before saying it is directly in the verse?

## Card H — teacher summary, *not a hadith quotation*

**Source label:** Hadith report attributed to Ibn ʿUmar, **Sahih al-Bukhari 8** (Belief, Book 2, in-book Hadith 1). [External report](https://sunnah.com/bukhari/2).

**Teacher-created summary:** The report describes five foundations of Islam: shahādah (testimony of faith), ṣalāh (prayer), zakāh (obligatory giving), ṣawm (fasting in Ramadan), and ḥajj (pilgrimage to Makkah). This is a child-facing summary of the located report, **not a direct quotation** and not an independent authenticated grading exercise.

**Question to investigate:** Which two pieces of metadata would another reader need to find this report again?

## Card S — **invented classroom scenario**, not a religious source

During a drawing task, Sam cannot find a pencil. A classmate has two pencils. The classmate offers one spare pencil and asks Sam to return it after the activity.

**Source label:** Original fictional teaching scenario, SC01. It is neither a Qur'anic passage nor a hadith, and does not claim the Prophet said or did anything.

**Question to investigate:** Describe a respectful response. Is the response written in Card Q or Card H, or are you *applying your own reasoning* to the classroom situation? Explain the difference.

Introduce the terms with pronunciation support as needed. The strip is a *teaching paraphrase* anchored to Card H and must not be represented as the exact hadith words. This course never requires a child to perform the practices to pass an academic assessment.

## Teacher response models

- **Q vs H vs S:** Q names a Qur'anic surah and ayah; H names a hadith collection and report number; S explicitly names its creator and fictional role. A source **label** identifies where to check a claim; it does not make a paraphrase the source text.
- **Five pillars:** Correct five matches are the ones in the strip. An error such as calling ordinary sharing `zakāh` must be explained: an invented generous action does not by itself establish the formal legal category.
- **Card Q:** Accept a child's clear paraphrase of the *teacher summary* about getting to know other peoples, with a distinction between what the card says and what the Qur'anic original itself says.
- **Card S:** Accept an age-appropriate justification such as "I could offer a pencil and speak kindly because that helps Sam" **labeled as an inference from the invented situation**. Do not put these words into the Prophet's mouth or claim Card Q commands this exact scenario.
- **Source limits:** A collection number locates a hadith. Authenticity, exact translation, commentary and disputed legal implications need independent qualified review.

## Before classroom use

Check both external source locators, select authorized translations/references, review language and doctrinal suitability with a qualified teacher, and record an appropriate supporting PR/review. See [source ledger](../../source-review.md) and [unit check](../../draft-assessments/unit-check.md).
