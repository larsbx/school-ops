## Aim and boundaries

This is a **substantive worked authoring example**, rather than a replacement for the 104 planning entries or an adopted course. It teaches how to tell a Qur'anic passage from a named hadith source and from a fictional classroom scenario; names the five pillars; and helps learners explain ethical inferences without pretending a summary is scripture or a report is independently authenticated.

There is no assessment of religious sincerity, observance, family practice or doctrinal adherence. The draft assumes no Arabic fluency, recitation ability or prior practice. The text selection, translation, school/community approach, language access, source suitability and time budget require decisions before delivery. Sīrah and juristic comparisons are later curriculum proposals, **not covered by the authored lessons**.

## Prototype outcomes (local labels only)

| Local key | Observable goal | Prototype evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IS2-P01 | Given three labeled cards, distinguish a Qur'anic citation, a hadith collection report, and an original classroom scenario, including which metadata enables source lookup. | [Unit check](draft-assessments/unit-check.md), tasks 1 and 2 |
| IS2-P02 | Match at least 4/5 named pillars to accurate beginner descriptions, explaining that the source is a named hadith report. | [Unit check](draft-assessments/unit-check.md), task 3 |
| IS2-P03 | Given a teacher summary of Qur'an 49:13, describe the idea of peoples knowing one another, without claiming the teacher's words are a Qur'anic quotation. | [Unit check](draft-assessments/unit-check.md), task 4 |
| IS2-P04 | Given an invented classroom situation, identify a respectful action, justify it using a labeled source or a clearly labeled personal reason, and explain the distinction. | [Unit check](draft-assessments/unit-check.md), task 5 |

These are authoring-level evidence targets; outcomes are **not adopted** until a reviewed course scope exists. No success criterion for devotion or private religious behavior is permitted.
