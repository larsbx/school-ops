# Sky records — learner practice and entry (DRAFT; no answers)

This is an original practice sheet for Unit 5 (U-MATH-001-05). Names and dates below are **fictional classroom examples**, **not** actual historical Moon or sky data. Use oral, tactile, drawn, written or scribed responses with the same reasoning goal. The teacher holds the answer key separately; no key or link to a key is included here. This learner-facing page is not the independent transfer assessment.

## Entry E1–E4

**E1.** On a strip with one box per date, start at **March 3** and move to **March 6**. How many one-day **intervals** pass? Mark March 3, 4, 5 and 6 and explain why counting four date labels is not the same as counting intervals.

**E2.** Sort each sentence into **record of what someone saw**, **no-view/missing evidence**, or **prediction**: (a) “I drew the bright shape I saw today.” (b) “Cloud covered the sky so I could not check.” (c) “I think the same shape will appear next Tuesday.”

**E3.** In this **invented** table, find one row where the view was blocked and one where a Moon sighting was actually recorded.

| Fictional date (2026) | View of patch of sky | Moon recorded from that view? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| March 2 | clear view | no (not seen) |
| March 4 | blocked by cloud | **not checked** |
| March 7 | clear view | yes (seen) |

**E4.** A straight strip is exactly **8 short units** long. Place identical rods that each cover **2 short units**. How many rods cover the strip exactly, starting at one end with no gaps or overlaps? Explain why the count of rods is not eight.

## Practice P1–P6

Use the following **invented** rehearsal log, **not a record of what happened in the sky**. “Moon recorded: no” means **not seen from that view at that time**, never “the Moon did not exist” or “the Moon was absent from the entire sky.”

| Fictional date (2026) | Clock time | View | Moon recorded? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| March 2 | 16:00 | clear | no (not seen in view) |
| March 4 | 16:00 | blocked by cloud | not checked |
| March 7 | 16:00 | clear | yes (seen in view) |
| March 11 | 16:00 | clear | no (not seen in view) |

**P1 (record).** Which row is **missing information about the Moon** rather than a negative sighting? Explain.

**P2 (elapsed days).** How many day **intervals** separate March 2 and March 7? Show the counting steps, not merely the number of date labels.

**P3 (second interval).** How many days separate March 4 and March 11? Does the blocked view on March 4 alter the **date arithmetic**? Explain.

**P4 (make a table).** Count rows by **view condition**, then draw two equally scaled bars for “clear” and “blocked.” Write the numbers next to the bars.

**P5 (claim).** A peer says, “The Moon was absent on March 4 because we could not see it.” Is this conclusion supported? Replace it with a precise claim the log supports.

**P6 (reason / rhetoric).** The entries on March 4 and March 11 are **seven days apart**. Does this prove the sky repeats exactly each week? Explain one **calendar fact**, one **data limitation**, and what future evidence a physical-cycle claim would need.

## Lesson exits X1–X4

**X1.** “Today the sky was obstructed by a roof from our safe viewing position; I made no Moon observation.” Is this a sighting, a negative sighting, or a **no-view record**? Give a reason.

**X2.** A hypothetical student records two dates: April 5 and April 9. How many **one-day gaps** are between the dates? Explain whether both endpoint labels are gaps.

**X3.** A week has seven calendar days. Does this alone show that a Moon appearance repeats every seven days? State the difference between a conventional calendar grouping and an observed physical event.

**X4.** A made-up observation sheet has **3** “clear view” entries, **2** “view blocked” entries and **1** “view not attempted” entry. Make a labeled summary of all **6** entries; can it tell us whether the Moon was seen on either blocked date? Why?

All independent practice is **unscored draft rehearsal**. Do not copy the exposed answers into later assessment or claim a real observation from these examples.
