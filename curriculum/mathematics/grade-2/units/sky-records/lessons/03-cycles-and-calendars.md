# Episode S5-L3 — A calendar week is not an observed Moon cycle (DRAFT)

- **Unit/outcome:** [U-MATH-001-05](../README.md), O-MATH-001-010, with O011 preparation on prediction versus observed data.
- **Evidence before starting:** Episode 1's missing-data distinction, Episode 2's dated intervals, or equivalent demonstrated evidence.
- **Art/Trivium:** astronomy (physical phenomena tested by observations) and arithmetic (dates); **grammar** “week,” “calendar convention,” “pattern,” “physical recurrence,” “prediction”; **logic** counterexample to overclaim; **rhetoric** a concise, source-limited argument to another learner.
- **Provisional duration/materials:** 25 minutes; printed fictional records, two date strips, screen-reader-accessible text. Real sky-viewing and lunar image sourcing **not included**.
- **Independent exit:** X3 and a source-evidence explanation, with [teacher-only key](../teacher-guide.md#practice-and-lesson-exit-keys).

## Teaching episode

| Phase | Minutes | Actual task / teacher move |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Recall | 4 | Read a blank log entry labeled “not checked.” Ask which moon-appearance facts it contains (none) and which date fact it contains (its date). |
| Grammar/model | 6 | Compare a seven-day segment on a date strip to a **hypothetical** event log. The conventional label “one week” names 7 calendar days; it does **not** supply 7 days of comparable sky observations. |
| Guided investigation | 7 | Use **P6**: March 4 → March 11 is seven days, but March 4 was blocked; ask if any Moon appearance was observed twice (no). Contrast supported claim (“these two dates are one week apart”) with unsupported (“the sky repeats weekly”). |
| Dialogue | 4 | Give a counterexample: two observations on consecutive Mondays may show different appearances. They remain one calendar week apart. Ask the pupil which evidence would need independent checking (dated comparable views over multiple intervals). This is a **logical scenario**, not a claim about any historical Monday. |
| Independent | 4 | Answer **X3** and tell a partner the distinction through words, drawing or AAC without model wording in view. |

## Model, feedback and progression

**Common error:** “week means seven days, so the Moon repeats every week.” Return to the difference between definitions of calendar intervals and empirical recurrence. **Alternate mistake:** “we know the physical cycle is not seven days.” The lesson only shows that calendar arithmetic alone cannot **prove** a period; the actual astronomical period is not established here and would require reviewed sources/records.

**Candidate exit criterion:** explicitly state that a seven-day week is a calendar convention and does **not** by itself demonstrate a repeat in Moon appearance. Include what kind of dated observation could start testing the claim. Do not mark a learner's personal astronomical belief; assess only the evidence distinction.
