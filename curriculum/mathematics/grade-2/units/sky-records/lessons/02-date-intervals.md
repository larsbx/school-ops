# Episode S5-L2 — Dates, gaps and measured intervals (DRAFT)

- **Unit/outcome:** [U-MATH-001-05](../README.md), O-MATH-001-010; revisit O-MATH-001-002 count-steps and Unit 4's O008 equal units.
- **Evidence before starting:** E1 day-gap count and E4 equal-unit check passed or repaired with a fresh task. One day interval is a uniform counting unit; the *label* on an endpoint is not an interval.
- **Art/Trivium:** astronomy's dated-log mathematics; **grammar** “day,” “date,” “interval”; **logic** distinguishes four labels from three gaps; **rhetoric** uses a dated strip others can reproduce.
- **Provisional duration/materials:** 25 minutes; date strip within one month, tactile strips or numbered boxes, fictional [P-table](../practice-learner.md#practice-p1p6), pointer/scribe/AAC allowed.
- **Independent exit:** X2, with [teacher-only answer](../teacher-guide.md#practice-and-lesson-exit-keys).

## Teaching episode

| Phase | Minutes | Actual task / teacher move |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Prerequisite | 4 | Walk E1's March 3 → March 6 three day gaps with finger, not with counted endpoint labels. Probe why each gap is one day. |
| Model | 5 | Model a **different** interval, March 1 → March 3: draw March 1,2,3, count **two** connecting segments and attach unit “days.” Ask if a missed record on March 2 changes the number of calendar days (it does not). |
| Guided | 7 | Solve **P2 and P3** from the fictional table. March 2 → 7 is **5** day gaps; March 4 → 11 is **7** even though March 4 has no view. Hide teacher's completed strip for second example; learner draws own steps. |
| Reason/counterexample | 5 | Compare two statements: “March 4 to 7 = four date labels” versus “March 4 to 7 = three day intervals.” Have learner reject the first as a measurement and state the difference. |
| Independent | 4 | Answer **X2** using a fresh April pair. Teacher records gap count, unit and reason separately. |

## Model, feedback and progression

**Incorrect answer 5 for April 5 → 9:** likely counted both endpoints; mark five dates, then four lines between successive dates. **Incorrect answer 3:** maybe excluded one internal day; trace segments, one unit each. Read dates aloud if decoding is a barrier; do **not** supply the number of intervals. Stay within one month for this bounded route; crossing months and leap years are not assessed.

**Candidate exit criterion:** correct four day gaps *with* a labeled-unit explanation. If counting is not demonstrated, return to arithmetic E1 or equal-unit E4; a correct numeral without the unit is not full evidence.
