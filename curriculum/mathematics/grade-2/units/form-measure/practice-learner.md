# U-MATH-001-04 — Learner practice and entry prompts (DRAFT)

- [Unit and scope](README.md) · four [lessons](README.md#authoring-sequence) · **teacher key distributed separately**.
- Work with **identically sized unit-square tiles**, measured short rods (one short unit each), double rods (two short units each) and a line with equally spaced labeled ticks.
- Show the model and explain *how* you know; a pointer, speech, diagram, raised-grid response or an adult recording your own response is acceptable.
- **These original examples are public authoring material; a new, unexposed equivalent is needed for an independent progress decision.** No time limit.

## Entry

**E1. Count and compare.** Lay out 8 equal counters and count them once each. Next to them lay out 6. Which group has more? Show matched pairs to explain why.

**E2. Build equal-square groups.** With six equal-size square tiles, make a rectangle with 2 rows of 3. Rearrange the *same* tiles in 1 row of 6. State the count before and after and explain if any tile was added or lost.

**E3. Marked spaces.** On a line with *equal gaps* labeled 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, point to 2 and 5. How many gaps (unit steps) lie between them? Trace each.

**E4. Cover a length.** A strip is six short-unit rods long. Beginning at its left endpoint, cover the strip with six identical short-unit rods without leaving gaps or overlapping them. Report the measured length with its unit.

## Guided lesson investigations G1–G8

**G1 (L1).** Build 2 rows of 4 equal square tiles and then 4 rows of 2 from the same tiles. How many tiles in each? Tell a partner why the total is unchanged.

**G2 (L1).** Compare a 2-row, 3-column rectangle of equal squares with a 1-row, 6-column rectangle of the *same-size* squares. Sam says, “Different outline means different number of squares.” Test Sam's claim by counting.

**G3 (L2).** Cover a strip 10 short units long with 1-short-unit rods. How many? Then cover the same strip with 2-short-unit rods. How many? Label both units.

**G4 (L2).** Mina covers a strip 8 short units long using three 2-short-unit rods laid end-to-end, leaving a gap at the right. Has Mina finished measuring? Show what she must change.

**G5 (L3).** On a line with equal gaps and ticks 0–12, start at 3. Move 4 *intervals* forward. Where do you stop? Trace gaps, not printed labels.

**G6 (L3).** On that line, move 5 intervals backward from 10. Separately, how far apart are ticks 4 and 9? Explain the counting method.

**G7 (L4).** Make a grid with 2 rows and 6 columns of equal squares. Place a vertical partition after column 3. How many squares are on either side? Are these equal shares? Why?

**G8 (L4).** In a 3-row, 4-column grid of equal squares, partition after column 1. Count squares on both sides. Is any two-part split necessarily equal? Give a counterexample.

## Lesson exits

**L1-X1 (O012 — forms).** Compare an arrangement of **4 rows of 2 equal square tiles** with **1 row of 8** same-size tiles. Give each count, say whether the outlines are the same, and prove or disprove “different outlines imply different square counts.”

**L2-X1 (O008 — length).** One strip is exactly **10 short units** long. Measure it with rods that are **2 short units** each. Report the number of *double rods*, then report the equivalent number of *short rods* and explain the difference.

**L3-X1 (O009 — intervals).** On a uniformly spaced number line labeled 0–12, start at 5, move **4 intervals forward and 2 intervals backward**. Report the final tick. Separately count the distance (number of gaps) from 2 to the final tick. Show your step route.

**L4-X1 (O012 — equal partitions).** A tiled whole has **2 rows and 6 columns** of identical squares. A divider after column 2 gives two pieces. Are these equal square shares? Move the divider if necessary to make *two equal square shares*; state both counts and justify your claim.
