# G4-L4 — Make and justify equal shares of a tiled whole

- **Unit:** [U-MATH-001-04](../README.md) · **status: draft** · **provisional 25 minutes**.
- **Art and outcome:** geometry and arithmetic; O-MATH-001-012 (*equal-square shares*); review O008/009 vocabulary only.
- **Entry:** E1–E2 and L1 square-tile counting; E4 if size of square units is uncertain.
- **Materials:** 3×4 grid of twelve equal squares, movable tile outlines, colored or tactile partition separators.
- **Observable goal:** divide a 12-square whole into two regions of **6 equal-size squares** each, compare with a 3-and-9 split and explain why a number of groups alone does not prove equality.
- **Grammar:** whole, part, equal share, unit square, region, row, column, partition.
- **Logic:** reject “two groups always means two equal groups”; demonstrate the counterexample 3 vs 9.
- **Rhetoric:** present an equal partition and convince a peer by one-to-one square counts and identical unit size.

## Instruction and evidence

1. **Retrieve (3 min):** verify 3 rows of 4 = 12 *equal-size square units*; learners may count individually rather than multiply.
2. **Model (7 min):** separate the 3×4 grid after column 2: each side 3 rows × 2 columns, **6 square units**. Explain equality in counted same-size unit squares.
3. **Refute (5 min):** split the grid after column 1: left 3 squares, right 9; it is two pieces but *not* two equal-area shares. Ask for a reason accessible to a partner.
4. **Fade (5 min):** without the worked split, learner finds a second partition *of the original 3×4 grid*: take all four squares in the top row plus the left two in the middle row as one 6-square part; the remaining two middle-row squares and four bottom-row squares form the other 6-square part. Request an explanation of why the differently outlined parts have equal square counts.
5. **Independent exit (5 min):** [L4-X1](../practice-learner.md#lesson-exits) with separate [teacher key](../practice-teacher.md#lesson-exit-keys).

## Feedback

If pieces look equal but have different unit-square counts, prompt a recount and identify where the comparison failed. If matching areas rely on different-sized tiles, substitute identical units. This is a preparation for rational-part vocabulary, not a claim that formal fractions, formulae or all forms of division have been taught.
