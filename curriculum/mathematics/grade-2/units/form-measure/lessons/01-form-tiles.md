# G4-L1 — Forms and equal-square coverings

- **Unit:** [U-MATH-001-04](../README.md) · **status: draft** · **provisional 25 minutes**.
- **Art and outcome:** geometry; O-MATH-001-012 (*form and equal-square arrangements only*).
- **Entry:** E1–E2 from the [unit learner sheet](../practice-learner.md); do not presume Unit 3 is taught.
- **Materials:** twelve identical square tiles, squared/tactile grid, large print or embossed alternatives.
- **Observable goal:** build and describe two arrays of six equal tiles, count each, explain why a change of form need not change the occupied number of equal squares.
- **Grammar:** tile, row, column, square, rectangle, equal-size square, covering.
- **Logic:** test “different-shaped arrays must contain different amounts”; distinguish equal tile count from identical outline.
- **Rhetoric:** give a partner enough labeled rows/columns and reasons to reconstruct and verify both arrangements.

## Instruction and evidence (teacher preparation only)

1. **Retrieve (3 min):** count six identical tiles and check the one-to-one count; for insecure counting, model a smaller route and do not assess the full goal.
2. **Model (6 min):** build two rows of three squares and one row of six squares. Cover every location with one same-size square, no overlap; in each count 6 squares. The shapes differ; their occupied unit-square counts do not. Do not assume this proves equal perimeter or area under unlike tile sizes.
3. **Guided reasoning (6 min):** learner builds three rows of two squares and counts 2+2+2=6. Ask whether “three rows” means “three squares”; correct with explicit units.
4. **Fade + partner retell (5 min):** hide model; learner communicates a different arrangement with its exact tile count. Partner reconstructs from the description; compare and revise.
5. **Independent exit (5 min):** [L1-X1](../practice-learner.md#lesson-exits-l1x1l4x1) without showing the answer; teacher checks [L1 key](../practice-teacher.md#lesson-exit-keys). Record independent vs supported privately.

## Feedback

If the number is correct but rows/columns cannot be explained, ask for a reconstruction with a partner, then use an unseen arrangement. If the learner counts outlines rather than unit squares, place a marker on each tile while counting. If the comparison silently swaps larger and smaller tiles, restart with identical unit squares. Do not infer formal area measurement from this introductory tile-count task.
