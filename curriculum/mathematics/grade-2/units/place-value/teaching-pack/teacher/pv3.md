# PV3 teacher route - hundreds, tens and ones

Grade 2 • C-MATH-001 / U-MATH-001-01 • draft • range 0–999

Sequence: M01 A/B (-01), hundreds (-02), number forms (-03), counting (-04),
comparison (-05), unit check (-06). Core estimate: 50 + 25 + 25 + 25 + 25 + 25
= 175 minutes, plus entry and optional work below. PVX remains separately taught.

## Administration and evidence contract

Start with A-MATH-001-01 (10–15 additional minutes), using its separate learner
sheet and item-specific criteria. Verify M01's twelve-counter prerequisite.
State the route from actual entry and lesson evidence, not grade alone.
No learner result or actual pace is assumed. Use the pack materials checklist;
establish the amount represented by each grouped object before using it as a token.

The sequence uses M01 two-cycle communication, then worked-example fading in
number forms, retrieval and concrete boundary modeling in counting, and a
claim/counterexample investigation in comparison. Complete models come before
supported attempts and blank independent sheets. Fading removes solution cues,
not manipulatives, read-aloud, scribing or explanation criteria. A teacher can
be the partner; no compulsory online practice or peer availability is assumed.

For lessons 2–5 use 3 minutes retrieval, 5 modeling, 7 guided practice, 7 independent
work and 3 exit/check. Split or extend when needed. Independent sheets revisit
some guided claims for practice; these are formative, not fresh summative evidence.
Give substantive help when needed and recognize the resulting work as supported.
All prompt parts have keys below or in the explicitly linked M01/assessment key.
These are authoring estimates, not a calendar or a required performance speed.

## Lesson 1 - L-MATH-001-01-01 using M01

Outcomes: O-MATH-001-001 tens/ones component; preparatory 002–003.
Prerequisite: count twelve counters one-to-one, name one counter as one-unit.
Materials: enough counters/base-ten equivalents for 99, blank Tens/Ones chart,
separate partner cards, paper and pencil. Primary art: arithmetic grouping.

Use the merged [M01 teacher guide](../../two-cycle/teacher.md) with its unchanged
[learner book](../../two-cycle/learner.md), [workbook](../../two-cycle/workbook.md)
and separate cards. Its scripts and complete A1–B4, W1–W8/card keys are authoritative
and included in the teacher print edition. Cycle A models 14, reconstructs groups,
and ends with 27; Cycle B unpacks 21 and ends with two models of 46. Grammar names
the counted one-unit and grouped ten; logic conserves the collection; rhetoric
asks a partner to reconstruct and inspect the explanation.

Use A4/B4 criteria: accurate amount and labels, conservation reason and explanation
another person can follow. Failure to count requires smaller collection instruction;
unit confusion requires opening a ten; zero confusion requires contrasting grouped
tens with an empty collection. Continue only after evidence in this component.
Do not read this as whole outcome-001 success. The optional A-MATH-001-04 review
has four tasks and a separate teacher key, linked in the pack and included in print.
Core time is 25 + 25 = 50; workbook 10–16 and formative review 5–10 are additional.

## Lesson 2 - L-MATH-001-01-02: hundreds and zero positions

Outcomes: O-MATH-001-001. Prerequisite: M01 conserving ten/ones exchange and zero
ones explanation. Materials: established ten-groups, hundred-groups with inspectable
contents, enough base-ten equivalents for 999, blank H/T/O chart, paper, pencil,
[learner sheet](../learner/pv3-02.md). Primary art: arithmetic decimal grouping.

Retrieve 40. Count ten tens as 10, 20, ... , 100 and exchange those tens for one
hundred; reopen the hundred to show the same amount. Compare 100 and 200.
Guided work: build 140, 305, 350; in 140 unpack one hundred into ten tens,
then one ten into ten ones. Keep the entire collection at each stage; never
count a bundle and its contents twice. Use sheet 01–03 for independent work;
04 is the exit. Grammar distinguishes digit and place amount; logic tests the
same-digits claim and conserves exchanges; rhetoric explains zero tens to a partner.

### Complete lesson and learner key

Retrieval: 40 = 4 tens. Ten tens = 100 ones = 1 hundred; 200 = 2 hundreds.
Guided: 140 = 1 hundred + 4 tens + 0 ones; 305 = 3 hundreds + 0 tens + 5 ones;
350 = 3 hundreds + 5 tens + 0 ones.

- P-PV3-02-01: 406 = 4 hundreds + 0 tens + 6 ones; zero tens means no extra tens. 230 = 2 hundreds + 3 tens + 0 ones; zero ones means no extra singles. Neither zero means the whole collection is empty.
- P-PV3-02-02: 140 = 1 hundred + 4 tens + 0 ones = 14 tens + 0 ones = 13 tens + 10 ones. Each exchange substitutes equal-valued groups; all 140 ones remain.
- P-PV3-02-03: false; 305 = 300 + 5, whereas 350 = 300 + 50. The 5 contributes five ones or five tens respectively, so the amounts differ.
- P-PV3-02-04: 402 = 4 hundreds + 0 tens + 2 ones; zero records no extra tens. Ten tens and one hundred both contain 100 ones, so exchange changes groups without changing amount.

Success check: correct models and zero explanations for all asked positions,
every reverse exchange shown, and ten tens as a conserved hundred. Return to
open groups if a token is named without its amount; do not advance based only
on writing a three-digit numeral. This does not teach or certify a thousand.

## Lesson 3 - L-MATH-001-01-03: number forms

Outcomes: O-MATH-001-003; review 001. Prerequisite: lesson 2's hundred/tens exchange
and zero positions. Materials: H/T/O models, blank chart, paper, pencil and
[learner sheet](../learner/pv3-03.md). Primary art: arithmetic representation.

Retrieve 305. Model 257, saying "two hundred fifty-seven," and connect 200 + 50 + 7
to the labeled groups. Guided work: 340 and 608. Test 608 = 60 + 8 by building both
amounts before revealing the correction. Remove the completed model for independent
sheet items 01/03/04; item 02 is the exit. Grammar names the forms; logic tests a
place-unit mismatch; rhetoric explains agreement among name, numeral, sum and model.

### Complete lesson and learner key

Retrieval: 305 = 3 hundreds + 0 tens + 5 ones, three hundred five.
Model: 257 = 200 + 50 + 7; 2 hundreds + 5 tens + 7 ones.
Guided: 340, three hundred forty, 300 + 40; 608, six hundred eight, 600 + 8.

- P-PV3-03-01: 503 = 500 + 3; five hundred three; model 5 hundreds, 0 tens, 3 ones.
- P-PV3-03-02: four hundred sixty; 400 + 60; the 6 contributes six tens, 60; zero means no extra ones.
- P-PV3-03-03: false; 60 + 8 = 68, whereas 608 = 600 + 8. The 6 in 608 records hundreds, not tens.
- P-PV3-03-04: three hundred forty; 300 + 40; 3 hundreds and 4 tens give the same 340 one-units in all forms.

Success check: all requested representations agree, each digit's contribution
is named accurately, and the zero/unit reasoning is clear. Spoken names and spelling
support are allowed; optional zero sum terms do not change correctness. Return to
the physical groups on a mismatch. The optional thousand activity is outside this core.

## Lesson 4 - L-MATH-001-01-04: steps and hundreds boundary

Outcomes: O-MATH-001-002; review 001/003. Prerequisite: read the amounts and explain
both hundred/tens and ten/ones exchanges. Materials: groups, blank chart, paper,
pencil and [learner sheet](../learner/pv3-04.md). Primary art: arithmetic repeated addition.

Retrieve 98 through 102 with models. At 99 add one: 9 tens + 10 ones; exchange to
10 tens, then to one hundred. Model four terms by tens from 230. Guided work:
hundreds from 500 to 900, naming one hundred as the step. Use sheet 01/04
independently; 02/03 for exit and explanation. Grammar defines start, term and
step; logic separates addition from conserving exchanges; rhetoric teaches the
rule with a chart. Starting values count as first terms, endpoints are included.

### Complete lesson and learner key

Retrieval: 98, 99, 100, 101, 102. Model: 230, 240, 250, 260; add one ten each time.
Guided: 500, 600, 700, 800, 900; add one hundred each time.

- P-PV3-04-01: 95, 100, 105, 110, 115; step five.
- P-PV3-04-02: 198, 199, 200, 201, 202. Before: 1 hundred + 9 tens + 9 ones. Add one to obtain 1 hundred + 9 tens + 10 ones; exchange to 1 hundred + 10 tens + 0 ones; then to 2 hundreds + 0 tens + 0 ones. Exchanges conserve the new 200 after addition.
- P-PV3-04-03: 390, 400, 410. Adding one ten to 3 hundreds + 9 tens gives 3 hundreds + 10 tens; exchange to 4 hundreds. Ones remain zero.
- P-PV3-04-04: 500, 600, 700, 800, 900; each step adds one hundred, equivalent to ten tens or one hundred ones.

Success check: all terms/steps correct with requested models, every exchange and
a preservation reason. A recited list alone is insufficient. Rebuild a shorter
boundary span if needed before progression. The lesson's thousand boundary activity
requires separate teaching and is excluded here.

## Lesson 5 - L-MATH-001-01-05: comparison

Outcomes: O-MATH-001-004; review 001/003. Prerequisite: connect three-digit numerals
to grouped amounts. Materials: H/T/O models, blank chart, paper, pencil and
[learner sheet](../learner/pv3-05.md). Primary art: arithmetic order.

Retrieve 204 and 240. Model 415 versus 451: match hundreds, then compare tens.
Guided work: 390 versus 409 and 600 versus 600. Test the irrelevant-digit argument
with groups. Use sheet 01/03/04 independently; 02 is the exit. Grammar reads
complete relation statements; logic explains why the first differing place controls
order; rhetoric states claim, model and reason for a recipient.

### Complete lesson and learner key

Retrieval: 204 = 2 hundreds + 0 tens + 4 ones; 240 = 2 hundreds + 4 tens + 0 ones.
Model: 415 < 451; hundreds agree, one ten is less than five tens.
Guided: 390 < 409; three hundreds are less than four; 600 = 600.

- P-PV3-05-01: 204 < 240 < 420. First pair: hundreds agree and zero tens are less than four tens. Second: two hundreds are less than four hundreds.
- P-PV3-05-02: 708 < 780; hundreds agree, zero tens are less than eight tens. 659 > 599; six hundreds exceed five hundreds.
- P-PV3-05-03: claim false; 390 < 409. Hundreds differ first; the larger tens digit of 390 cannot outweigh the extra hundred in 409.
- P-PV3-05-04: 600 = 600; same six hundreds, zero tens and zero ones in both models.

Success check: symbols, complete relations and first-differing-place/equality
reasons agree for every requested comparison. Restore matched place-value models
on irrelevant-digit reasoning. Use PV3 unit evidence only after the route's
relationships have been taught, without requiring the optional PVX extension.

## Lesson 6 - L-MATH-001-01-06: selected-route evidence

Outcomes: O-MATH-001-001–004 within the taught route. Materials: selected learner
form, working paper, counters/base-ten blocks, blank chart; no calculator.
Prerequisite: the route's preceding lesson models and explanations, or equivalent
privately documented evidence. Do not assess PVX without its separate instruction.

Retrieve unit names and symbols for 3 minutes without exposing assessment answers.
Distribute only the selected A-MATH-001-02 learner form. Allow 15–20 minutes,
extra time or split sittings. Complete a 2-minute teach-back if useful, but inspect
all requested reasons during the assessment, not only that final sample.
No speed threshold applies. Teacher print editions include the full existing
assessment key; do not distribute those editions to learners.

Read directions without answer cues; accept speech, drawing, pointing and scribing.
Accept customary "and" in number names and omit zero terms in sums if accurate.
Record substantive teaching prompts as support. Every subpart must meet its
outcome criterion: model/units/exchange/zero in items 01–03 plus composition in
04; all sequences, steps and boundary reasons in 04–06; conversions and model
connection in 07–09; comparison symbols and place-value reasons in 10–12.
A recited sequence alone does not establish the conservation criterion in item 04.
Addition changes the amount; subsequent equal-valued exchanges conserve the new amount.

Grammar checks names and notation; logic checks invariant/step/comparison reasons;
rhetoric checks whether the explanation connects claim, model and reason for another
person. Inspect independently / supported / needs review / not assessed per outcome
and taught scope privately. Do not sum these into a course pass mark.

Use a fresh inspected bank 1.0.1 conceptual form when full applications were practiced
or keys exposed. Changing seeds alone does not establish freshness. Keep manifests
and all keys separate. Timed bank drills remain optional fluency work outside the
core instruction/assessment budget, and never replace model/reason evidence.

## Follow-up decisions by relationship

| Evidence gap | Revisit | Fresh application / next decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One-unit, grouped unit or reverse exchange (001) | M01; PV3 also lesson 2 | Open and repack equal-valued groups; show each stage with a different amount before continuing |
| Zero position (001/003) | M01 zero ones; PV3 lesson 2 zero tens | Contrast an empty position with an empty collection; name remaining groups |
| Boundary or repeated step (001/002) | Lesson 4 objects and chart | Add the one or grouped step first, then conserve that new amount through exchanges; check a different boundary |
| Name, numeral or sum mismatch (003) | Lesson 3 concrete-to-symbol connection | Use a new keyed amount and connect each place to its unit; spelling support remains available |
| Irrelevant digit used for comparison (004) | Lesson 5 matched higher places | Build two fresh keyed amounts; inspect the first differing place or equality |
| Correct result without model/reason | Relevant lesson explanation task | Ask for a demonstration; recognize teaching prompts as support and recheck afterward |

Proceed in the checked scope only after every part of the relevant outcome group
is accurate with its model and reason. Supported evidence informs teaching; obtain
fresh independent application before calling that relationship independent. Keep
unassessed content pending. No duration, confidence rating or exercise count proves
understanding. Private records include route/form ID, support, outcome judgments and
follow-up; the shared repository receives only curriculum changes.

## Optional PVX after PV3

Use the original lesson-3 thousand activity and lesson-4 boundary activity linked
from the pack index, with their actual independent practice and complete keys.
Teacher print editions append those unchanged lesson records for reference.
Prerequisite: model 200 and explain the hundred-to-ten relationship, then demonstrate
thousand grouping and all three boundary exchanges before administering PVX.
Materials: ten established hundreds, a thousand-group, blank four-column chart.
Additional instruction is 25 + 10–15 minutes; assessment adds 5–10, with flexibility.
Assess only grouping/representation of 1,000 and the 996–1,000 boundary, not general
four-digit calculations or comparison. PVX-01 and -02 have their existing full
key in A-MATH-001-02. Omit entirely when untaught; record pending rather than failed.
