## Prerequisites

Read the values used and understand the counted unit. Choose a smaller range
when necessary; the repeated-step relationship remains the same.

## Grammar

Name starting value, step, sequence, and next value. State explicitly whether
the first value is included in the requested number of terms.

## Logic

Predict what follows 99 and 199 and justify the regrouping. Compare two sequences
with different steps and explain which counted unit changes.

## Rhetoric

Teach a counting pattern to another person, saying the rule as well as the values.

## Activities

1. Retrieval: count 98 through 102 using objects or a model (3 minutes).
2. Model: start at 230 and count by tens: 230, 240, 250, 260 (5 minutes).
3. Supported work: count by hundreds from 500 to 900; discuss boundaries (7 minutes).
4. Independent work: give five terms by fives starting at 95 (7 minutes).
5. Exit: count 198 through 202; give three terms by tens from 390 and explain the step (3 minutes).

## Teacher key and next step

- First count: 98, 99, 100, 101, 102.
- Hundreds: 500, 600, 700, 800, 900.
- Fives: 95, 100, 105, 110, 115, including the starting value.
- Exit: 198, 199, 200, 201, 202; 390, 400, 410.
- Seek an explained repeated step, not only a recited sequence.
- If a boundary causes difficulty, model the exchange and practice a shorter span.

## Two-digit route

Use these examples when ones/tens are the taught scope. Include the starting
value in every stated number of terms. Hundreds and the thousand boundary are
separate later work.

| Stage | Task and mathematical work | Minutes | Teacher key |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Retrieval | Count by ones from 38 through 42 | 3 | 38, 39, 40, 41, 42 |
| Model | Give four terms by tens starting at 20 | 5 | 20, 30, 40, 50; one ten added each time |
| Supported work | Give five terms by fives starting at 25 | 7 | 25, 30, 35, 40, 45; step five |
| Independent work | Give five terms by fives starting at 55 | 7 | 55, 60, 65, 70, 75 |
| Exit | Count 58 through 62; give three terms by tens starting at 46 and explain the step | 3 | 58, 59, 60, 61, 62; 46, 56, 66 |

Ask for the step and a model of the tens boundary, rather than only a recited
list. When the thousand grouping has subsequently been taught, a separate boundary
example is to count 993 through 1,000 and explain the exchanges at the endpoint:
993, 994, 995, 996, 997, 998, 999, 1,000. This is extension teaching, not part of
the two-digit route.
