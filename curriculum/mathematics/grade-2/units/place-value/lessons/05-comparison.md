# Equality, order, and a reason for comparison

- Lesson ID: L-MATH-001-01-05
- Unit: [U-MATH-001-01](../README.md)
- Intended learners: 2nd grade
- Status: draft
- Primary art: Arithmetic
- Course outcome focus: 004; review 001 (prefix `O-MATH-001-`)
- Time estimate: 25 minutes, adjustable after entry work
- Materials: counters/bundled sticks or base-ten blocks, paper, pencil, place-value chart
- Review: pending in [PR #1](https://github.com/larsbx/school-ops/pull/1)

## Prerequisites

Connect numerals to place-value amounts; review with smaller models if needed.

## Grammar

Name equal, greater, and less; connect those relations to =, >, and <.
Read the complete comparison aloud.

## Logic

Investigate the false claim that 390 is greater than 409 because 9 is greater
than 0. Identify the first differing place and show why it controls the comparison.

## Rhetoric

Explain a comparison by stating the claim, naming the relevant units, and giving
the reason another person can inspect.

## Activities

1. Retrieval: read and model 204 and 240 (3 minutes).
2. Model: compare 415 and 451 using hundreds, then tens (5 minutes).
3. Supported work: compare 390 with 409 and 600 with 600 (7 minutes).
4. Independent work: order 204, 240, and 420 and explain the order (7 minutes).
5. Exit: complete 708 __ 780 and 659 __ 599 with reasons (3 minutes).

## Teacher key and next step

- 415 < 451: hundreds equal; 1 ten is less than 5 tens.
- 390 < 409: 3 hundreds are less than 4 hundreds; 600 = 600.
- Increasing order: 204, 240, 420.
- Exit: 708 < 780; 659 > 599.
- Require the correct relation, symbol, and a place-value reason.
- If the reason relies on a single irrelevant digit, compare concrete models again.
