# Unit 6 — learner practice and entry sheet

**Draft original questions** for the existing `U-MATH-001-06`. A separate teacher-only answer sheet exists and must not accompany learner exports. **These are fixed instructional examples, not unseen future tests.** Speak, draw, point, sign or use AAC; read-aloud/scribing without supplying operations or reasons is allowed. Where a number line is requested, spacing must represent **equal units**. Tell what the quantities count. Use counters and base-ten rods when helpful. No timer.

## Prerequisite entry check (learner)

**E1 — Counting units.** Represent **48** counters as tens and ones. Add one *full ten* of the same counters. Write or say the new total and explain whether any ones disappeared.

**E2 — Joining counted units.** A table holds **6 counters** and another holds **7 counters**. How many counters in all? Build or draw a model and check.

**E3 — Equal steps.** On a number line with equal one-unit marks, start at **24**, move forward **7** steps. Mark the end. Why is the distance 7?

**E4 — Read only recorded rows.** This practice table gives **Day A: 4 cards; Day B: 7 cards; Day C: 4 cards**. Which day has the largest recorded count? Can you know Day D's count from the table? Explain.

## Guided and independent practice

### P1 — Independent regrouping

A box contains **75 paper cards** and another adds **28 paper cards**. How many cards in all? Show the ones, tens and any hundred. Explain an exchange that preserves the count. Check your total a second way.

### P2 — Number-line reconstruction

Start at **70** on an equally spaced number line. Move forward **25** equal units, then backward **13**. Label start, turning point and final point. What is the final number? Explain why your final mark is above 70 and below the turning point.

### P3 — Table and claim

A fictional collection has **First day: 7 recycled cards; Middle day: 11; Last day: 9**. Make a 3-row table, find the total across recorded rows, and find the middle-minus-first difference. A classmate says “The next day will have 13.” Does the given table establish that? Explain.

### P4 — Compare two options

You need **25 badges**. Option A contains **four packs of seven badges**. Option B contains **three packs of nine badges**. Assume any extra badge is unwanted and prices are not provided. Which options supply enough badges, and which leaves the fewest extras? Give a model or calculation for both and explain what the missing prices prevent you from concluding.

### P5 — Fresh guided two-digit comparison (instruction only)

There are **66 tiles** in one drawer and **27** in another. How many tiles total? Recompose tens and ones and check your answer.

### P6 — Fresh guided remaining-quantity story (instruction only)

A shelf has **118 books**; **49 books** are borrowed. How many remain? Explain an inverse check.

### P7 — Fresh guided recorded-count table (instruction only)

Three *invented* counts are A = **10**, B = **8**, C = **15** objects. Find total and C − B. Can this establish a value for D?

### P8 — Fresh guided pack choice (instruction only)

A group needs **24 cards**. Plan A is **two packs of 13**; Plan B is **three packs of 9**. Which is sufficient? Which leaves fewer extra cards? Compare **extra cards**, not number of packages.
