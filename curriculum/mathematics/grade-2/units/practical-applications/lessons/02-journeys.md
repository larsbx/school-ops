# Lesson 2 — A number-line journey with remaining places (teacher guide)

**Unit:** [U-MATH-001-06](../README.md) · **Status:** draft, not teaching-ready · **Outcomes:** 006 and 009 (007 only if regrouping independently shown) · **Arts:** arithmetic and number-line geometry · **Pattern:** test–teach–test · **Proposed duration:** 25 minutes.

**Prerequisite:** E1–E3 independent; Unit 4's authoring PR is not proof of number-line competence. **Materials:** large, equally spaced number line with labeled anchor tens and intermediate units, counters, paper/tactile equivalent. Adult can prepare a scale **without filling in answers**.

## Trivium and activity

**Grammar (4 min).** A *starting point* is the current count, an *increase* moves toward larger marks, a *decrease* toward smaller marks; *distance* between two points counts equal unit steps, not written labels.

**Logic/model (8 min).** “A hall has **125 seats**. **68** are occupied. How many are still free?” Show a counted-on jump from 68 to 125: +2 to 70, +30 to 100, +25 to 125, for **57 free seats**. Check 68 + 57 = 125. The *number line is a representation*, not a claim that a physical hall is one-dimensional. Mark the number line with a consistent scale: use 10-unit anchors, show the +2 and +5 unit endpoints exactly.

**Guided (6 min).** “A shelf had **104 books**. **38** were taken. How many remain?” Count on 38→40 (+2), 40→100 (+60), 100→104 (+4): **66 books**. Check 38 + 66 = 104. Ask whether a jump from 40 to 100 is 6 units or 60 units; it is 60.

**Rhetoric / Independent exit (7 min).** [P2](../practice-learner.md#p2--number-line-reconstruction): start at 70, move forward 25, back 13. **Key 82** after intermediate 95; explain why the final count is below 95 but above 70. The learner marks 70, 95, 82 on a uniform conceptual scale or uses an equal-step tactile model.

## Feedback/recheck

An answer without a scale/reason does not establish 009. For erroneous step size, model equal ten-step segments, then give a new smaller entry task; preserve access supports. For a learner requiring 0–99, use counts on a 0–99 line but record the full check's larger route as **not assessed**. No negative results or signed integer formalism is introduced.
