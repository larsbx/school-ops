# Lesson 4 — Defend a practical choice (teacher guide)

**Unit:** [U-MATH-001-06](../README.md) · **Status:** draft, not teaching-ready · **Outcomes:** 006, bounded 007; retrieve number-line/table reasons for 009/011 if entry work permits · **Art:** arithmetic applied to a design/count problem · **Pattern:** two-cycle mathematical communication · **Proposed duration:** 25 minutes.

**Prerequisites:** all E1–E4 independently rechecked, or provide individual remedial routes and postpone integrated exit. This is not an assessment of a child's budgeting or family purchasing choices. **Materials/access:** equal-sized paper tokens/packs, a blank grid, teacher-readable card text and speech/AAC/drawing/pointing response options.

## Two communication cycles

**Grammar/context (4 min).** *Pack size* = number of objects in one pack. *Packs* counts containers; *capacity* counts total objects. *Leftover* = capacity − needed. These are all counts, **not prices**. Assume all options available and no price difference; if those assumptions change, the preferred answer may change.

**Logic cycle A (8 min).** A project needs **26 art cards**. Plan A supplies **three packs of 9**; Plan B supplies **two packs of 14**. Calculate A = 27 (1 spare), B = 28 (2 spare). **Both are sufficient; A gives the fewest spare cards** among stated options. Counterexample to “the fewer packs always give fewer extras”: B uses fewer packs but has more spare cards.

**Rhetoric cycle B (6 min).** Give a partner only the statement “Choose A, because ...”; partner must reconstruct 3×9 = 27 and 27 − 26 = 1. Then have partner challenge it: “What if B were cheaper?” Answer: without prices, the scenario supports minimal waste, not minimal cost.

**Independent exit (7 min).** [P4](../practice-learner.md#p4--compare-two-options) offers **25 badges**, option A 4×7, B 3×9. **Key:** A 28 (3 extras), B 27 (2 extras), both sufficient, **B minimizes extras** under stated assumption. Learner must separately name groups, count per group, total, extras and assumption.

## Decision, feedback and limitations

Record explanation and units separately from bare result. A learner who selects the option with fewer *packs* without counting capacity needs fresh equal-group practice, not a higher arithmetic threshold. Reassess with independent quantities after correction. The lesson is original teaching material, **not a purchase recommendation**; no genuine prices, costs, measurements, marketplace conditions, sky observations or learner records are supplied.
