# Unit 3 — original diagnostic and guided learner practice

**Draft · Grade 2 · Arithmetic · Not an approved assessment.** Write, speak, point, arrange counters, use a tactile board, draw, or respond with AAC. Ask an adult to read the directions if helpful. Show how you know, not only a number. The suggested number route is 0–24, or a separately adapted 0–12 review route.

## Entry diagnostic (do not score by average)

### E1 — Count and conserve
Take **12** counters. Count them; lay them in a line, then in two rows. State the total after rearranging, and explain why it did or did not change.

### E2 — Equal versus unequal
Compare **three groups containing 4, 4, 4 counters** with **three groups containing 5, 4, 3 counters**. Which is equal-group sharing? Point to or name the evidence.

### E3 — Composition and pairing
Show **6 + 6** with counters; state the total. Make disjoint pairs from that same collection; state whether an unpaired counter remains.

## Episode 1 — build and reject groups

### P1 — Give all three units
Draw **3 equal groups of 4** counters. How many groups, how many in each group, and how many counters altogether? Explain how you checked.

### P2 — Same amount, different arrangement
Arrange **12** counters into **4** equal groups, then into **3** equal groups. Write or explain the size of each group for both arrangements. Did any counters appear or disappear?

### P3 — Detect an error
A drawing shows groups containing **4, 4 and 3** counters. Someone calls it “three equal groups of four.” Explain what is wrong and give the actual total.

## Episode 2 — what is unknown?

### P4 — Sharing with a fixed number of groups
Share **15** counters equally among **5** boxes. How many counters must be in **each box**? Draw the boxes and check by recombining.

### P5 — Grouping with a fixed group size
Put **18** counters into groups of **3**. How many **groups** can you make? Explain why every counter was used exactly once.

### P6 — Leftover
Try to share **11** whole counters evenly between **2** boxes. Explain whether equal whole-number groups are possible and what remains.

## Episode 3 — invert the operation

### P7 — Double
Show double **8** with two equal collections. Give the total and explain why they are two equal copies.

### P8 — Halve
Share **18** counters between **2** equal boxes. State the count in one half and check by putting the two halves together.

After guided practice, show your reasoning for a **new** arrangement chosen by your teacher. Explain how every counter is accounted for.
