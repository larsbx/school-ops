# Episode 1 — Make and explain equal groups

- Lesson ID: **L-MATH-001-03-01**; **status: draft**; arithmetic / O-MATH-001-013 (equal grouping only).
- Prerequisite: Unit 3 E1 and E2 observed or remediated; do not infer from a grade label.
- Materials: 24 counters, grid or boxes, pencil; proposed 25 minutes, adjustable. Number route 0–24 or 0–12.

## Trivium actions

**Grammar:** a *group* is a separate set; *groups of equal size* each have the same number of objects. Distinguish “3 groups” from “4 in each group” and from “12 in all.” The total is counted in individual one-units, not in group-units.

**Logic:** moving counters into new disjoint rows cannot change the total; a group of 3, one of 4 and one of 5 is **not** “3 equal groups.” Test that claim by recounting each row.

**Rhetoric:** another learner must be able to reconstruct your arrangement from an oral, signed, drawn, tactile or written explanation giving number of groups, objects in each and total.

## Teaching and independently checkable work

1. **Model (6 min):** Place 12 counters in 3 boxes of 4. Explain “4 + 4 + 4 = 12” and why each counter appears exactly once. Rearrange into 4 boxes of 3; still 12. Do not equate *the arrangements* even though totals match.
2. **Guided inquiry (7 min):** Give 15 counters. Make 5 equal groups, then 3 equal groups. **Key:** 5 groups of 3, or 3 groups of 5; both count 15 and each uses every counter once.
3. **Counterexample (4 min):** Draw groups with counts 4, 4, 3 and ask if this is “3 equal groups of 4.” **Key:** no; the third has 3, the total is 11, and the claim would require 12.
4. **Independent practice/exit (8 min):** Use [P1–P3](../practice-learner.md); withhold the [teacher key](../practice-teacher.md). Required evidence: P1 and P2 matching counts/models plus a spoken/drawn reason; P3 correctly rejects unequal grouping. If incomplete, re-model 6–12 objects and use fresh quantities for reassessment.

Retain physical models, read-aloud, tactile boards and communication supports. Fade *answer cues*, not access. A prompted solution is supported practice, not independent evidence.
