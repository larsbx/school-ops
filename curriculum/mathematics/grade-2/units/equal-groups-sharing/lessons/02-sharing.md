# Episode 2 — Sharing and grouping ask different questions

- Lesson ID: **L-MATH-001-03-02**; **status: draft**; arithmetic / O-MATH-001-013 (sharing and grouping).
- Prerequisite: Unit 3 E1/E2 and independently represented disjoint equal groups in Episode 1.
- Materials: counters, numbered paper boxes, pencil, alternative tactile board; proposed 25 minutes, flexible.

## Trivium actions

**Grammar:** distinguish “How many *in each* group?” from “How many *groups*?” Name what is known and what remains unknown, and label the unit of the answer.

**Logic:** distribution succeeds only if every object is used and every container has the same amount. Check a solution by putting the groups back together. A leftover means there is no equal whole-object distribution under the stated conditions.

**Rhetoric:** describe the procedure and the reconstruction check without simply saying “divide”; the listener must know whether the answer counts objects per group or groups themselves.

## Teaching and independently checkable work

1. **Worked share (6 min):** Share 12 counters among 3 marked containers one at a time. **Key:** 4 counters per container; 4 + 4 + 4 = 12. Why did we not say “4 groups”? Because the question fixed the number of containers at 3.
2. **Guided group (7 min):** Place 18 counters into groups *of 3* (each group must have 3). **Key:** 6 groups, 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 18. The group size, not the group count, was fixed.
3. **Compare (4 min):** Ask “15 shared among 5 containers” versus “15 placed in groups of 5.” **Key:** 3 in each container in the first case; 3 groups in the second. Both total 15 but the answers refer to different units.
4. **Independent practice/exit (8 min):** Use [P4–P6](../practice-learner.md). **Key:** in [teacher guide](../practice-teacher.md). P4 requires model + size; P5 model + number of groups; P6 must state that one leftover prevents two *equal whole-number* groups. A numerical answer without the matching unit or reconstruction is incomplete.

If 0–24 is not demonstrated, repeat with 6 or 12 objects before trying 15/18. Fractional sharing is deferred; do not convert 11 shared between two into a fraction lesson.
