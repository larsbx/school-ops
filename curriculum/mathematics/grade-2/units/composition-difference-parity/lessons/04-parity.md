# Pairing and parity

- ID: L-MATH-001-02-04
- Status: draft
- Art: arithmetic
- Outcome: O-MATH-001-013 (pairing only)
- Prerequisite: evidence from lesson 03
- Materials: identical counters, paper and pencil
- Proposed time: 25 minutes, adjustable

## Grammar, logic and rhetoric

Name pair, leftover, even and odd. Test whether every object belongs to a pair. Explain why moving pairs around does not change whether one is left over.

## Teaching sequence and key

1. Model (6 min): Pair 12 counters into six disjoint pairs; none left, so even. Add one: six pairs and one leftover, so odd.
2. Guided completion (7 min): Pair 15 counters; classify and name the leftover. **Key:** Seven pairs and one leftover; odd.
3. Independent (8 min): distribute only the relevant part of the [learner copy](../learner-practice.md). Pair 18 counters. Rearrange the pairs and explain why the classification stays the same. **Key:** Nine disjoint pairs exhaust the 18 counters before and after rearranging; even.
4. Exit (4 min, flexible): Classify 17 by pairing and tell a partner why it differs from 18. **Key:** Eight pairs and one leftover; odd. One additional counter would complete a ninth pair.

## Evidence and response

Look for the keyed result, a matching model and the stated reason. If the result is right but the reason or model is missing, revisit the concrete example and use a fresh task. Fade solution cues only after a correct fresh response and reason; retain all access supports listed in the [unit](../README.md). Do not record a prompted solution as independent.
