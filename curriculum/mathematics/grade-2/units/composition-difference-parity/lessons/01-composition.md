# Parts and wholes

- ID: L-MATH-001-02-01
- Status: draft
- Art: arithmetic
- Outcome: O-MATH-001-005
- Prerequisite: unit entry check
- Materials: identical counters, paper and pencil
- Proposed time: 25 minutes, adjustable

## Grammar, logic and rhetoric

Name part, whole and equal. Explain that moving objects between parts keeps the whole fixed. Tell a partner how both arrangements count the same collection.

## Teaching sequence and key

1. Model (6 min): Join 6 and 3 counters: 6 + 3 = 9. Move one across the divider: 5 + 4 = 9. Count each part and the unchanged whole.
2. Guided completion (7 min): Build 7 + 4, then move one counter across. Name both parts and whole. **Key:** 7 + 4 = 6 + 5 = 11; no counters entered or left.
3. Independent (8 min): distribute only the relevant part of the [learner copy](../learner-practice.md). Split 13 counters into 8 and another part. Show a model and explain the missing part. **Key:** 5; 8 + 5 = 13. The two disjoint parts exhaust the whole.
4. Exit (4 min, flexible): Split 10 into two different pairs of parts; explain why both totals agree. **Key:** For example 6 + 4 and 7 + 3; both exhaust the same 10 counters.

## Evidence and response

Look for the keyed result, a matching model and the stated reason. If the result is right but the reason or model is missing, revisit the concrete example and use a fresh task. Fade solution cues only after a correct fresh response and reason; retain all access supports listed in the [unit](../README.md). Do not record a prompted solution as independent.
