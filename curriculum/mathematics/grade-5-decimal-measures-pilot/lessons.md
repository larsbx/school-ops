# Draft teaching episodes — arithmetic through the Trivium

Use the [entry routes](teacher-entry.md) first. Give the [learner practice](learner-practice.md) without its [key](teacher-practice.md); exits are independent with the same permitted access supports. Four illustrative budgets, not a timetable.

## Episode 1 — tenths and hundredths of a metre (P1)

| Work | Minutes |
| --- | --- |
| Grammar: label a strip 1 m, 10 tenths, 100 hundredths; build 0.36 m as 3 tenths and 6 hundredths | 6 |
| Logic: exchange 1 tenth for 10 hundredths; test 0.4 m = 0.40 m by counting 40 cm | 7 |
| Rhetoric: learner explains to an imaginary cutter why zero at the right of the hundredths notation does not change the length | 4 |
| Guided G1–G2 with feedback; fade the labelled columns on the second example | 5 |
| Independent X1 | 3 |

If a learner says 0.4 m is 4 cm, ask them to show four tenths on the strip; count 40 unit shares. If columns are needed for access, keep blank columns, not filled ones.

## Episode 2 — same whole, exact comparison (P2)

| Work | Minutes |
| --- | --- |
| Grammar: name numerator, denominator and same whole; build 3/5 m as 60/100 m | 5 |
| Logic: partition a metre into 20 equal lengths of 5 cm; compare 13/20 m with 0.6 m; 65 exceeds 60 | 7 |
| Rhetoric: justify comparison in centimetres and explain why comparing bare fractions of unequal strips is insufficient | 5 |
| Guided G3–G4, asking for equivalent shares rather than a calculator decimal | 5 |
| Independent X2 | 3 |

When a learner compares only numerators, make the denominator define the unit first. If the whole is different, calculate both actual lengths before deciding.

## Episode 3 — a length budget and its boundary (P3)

| Work | Minutes |
| --- | --- |
| Grammar: distinguish available length, used length and remainder; state that all lengths are exact for the exercise | 5 |
| Logic: model 1.40 m available, 0.65 m plus 1/2 m used, then 140 − 115 = 25 cm; test a required 25 cm piece against the remainder | 7 |
| Rhetoric: give a decision and explain why equality passes a rule of at least the required length | 4 |
| Guided G5–G6 using one unit consistently; check by adding used and remaining | 6 |
| Independent X3 | 3 |

Do not model an actual cutting machine; no blades or physical cutting required. If the learner mixes metres and centimetres, label the unit beside each quantity, then rewrite every term before adding.

## Episode 4 — defend or revise a decision (P4)

| Work | Minutes |
| --- | --- |
| Grammar: distinguish claim, calculation, condition and conclusion; read a fictional stock note | 5 |
| Logic: test “0.75 m is less than 0.8 m because 75 is less than 8” by using common units; identify the error in the quoted reason even though the conclusion is true | 7 |
| Rhetoric: compose a brief recommendation with evidence and a limit; exchange it with another learner or teacher for a check | 5 |
| Guided G7–G8: distinguish exact-model feasibility from an unrecorded cutting allowance | 5 |
| Independent X4 | 3 |

A correct choice with a false reason does not meet P4. Invite a corrected sentence, not a judgement about the speaker. If an allowance is not recorded, state a conditional answer rather than assuming a real-world fit.
