# Guided practice and independent exits — draft learner sheet

All lengths and workshop notes are fictional. Use 1 m = 100 cm. Each fraction with unit m refers to the same one-metre whole. Explain using a labelled drawing, numbers, speech or writing.

**G1.** Write 0.57 m as tenths plus hundredths, as a fraction of one metre with denominator 100, and as centimetres.

**G2.** A label says 0.9 m and another says 0.90 m. Decide if they match, then justify with centimetres.

**G3.** Compare 17/25 m with 0.7 m. Show equivalent hundredths for both.

**G4.** Which is longer, 3/4 of a 60 cm strip or 2/5 of a 100 cm strip? Name each whole and give both lengths.

**G5.** A workshop has 1.35 m of ribbon. A design uses 0.48 m and 3/5 m. How many centimetres remain? Check the sum.

**G6.** A second design has 1.25 m available and uses 0.70 m plus 1/4 m. It needs at least 30 cm left. Does it meet the rule? Show the boundary.

**G7.** Revise this claim: “0.42 m is longer than 0.6 m because 42 is greater than 6.” Explain why the digits alone are not enough.

**G8.** A plan has 1.10 m available. It needs a 0.65 m piece and a 0.45 m piece. Is the plan feasible in the exact model? A real workshop may lose some material when cutting; the allowance is not recorded. What can you say about real feasibility?

## Independent exits

**X1.** Explain 0.08 m as hundredths and centimetres. Is it equal to 0.8 m?

**X2.** Compare 19/20 m with 0.9 m and explain using equal shares.

**X3.** From 1.60 m, use 0.85 m and 1/2 m. Is at least 25 cm left? Check.

**X4.** A learner says half of 70 cm equals half of 90 cm because the fraction is identical. Write a correction with both lengths and the condition for equal halves.
