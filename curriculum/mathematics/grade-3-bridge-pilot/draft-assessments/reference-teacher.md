# Grade 3 arithmetic bridge — teacher-only reference answers and rubric (DRAFT)

**Keep apart from the learner form.** These are **complete model answers** to a fixed, published authoring example, not independent or secret assessment evidence. No learner has been tested; status remains **not ready**. The proposed criteria are component-specific and require approval before use. Accept equivalently correct spoken, tactile, braille, drawn, scribed or written demonstrations; assistance must not supply the mathematical decision.

| Task | Correct answer and explanatory evidence | Common error / feedback |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S1 | **5,006 = 5×1,000 + 0×100 + 0×10 + 6×1 = 5,000 + 6**. Hundreds and tens places each have zero of their named units; both zeros maintain position. | Writing 56 or 506 drops places; reconstruct labeled four-place pockets and retry with 6,009. |
| S2 | **2,480 < 2,804**. Thousands tie at 2; **hundreds 4 < 8**, so comparison decided there. | Comparing 80 and 4 out of position; align thousands/hundreds before comparing. |
| S3 | **False; 3,099 < 3,909**. Both have 3 thousands, then **0 hundreds < 9 hundreds**; “99” and “9” are not aligned place quantities. | Ask for place labels and a counterexample 3,020 versus 3,200. |
| S4 | **4×8 = 32** small squares. Left **4×3 = 12**, right **4×5 = 20**, sum **12 + 20 = 32**. The two sections cover all cells exactly once. | 4×3 + 5 omits fourfold count in right section; point to each row. |
| S5 | **6×3 = 18**. One-column strip **6×1 = 6**, two-column strip **6×2 = 12**, sum **6 + 12 = 18**. | The cut is across columns, not rows; label axes and re-lay out. |
| S6 | **False. 4×(2+3) = (4×2) + (4×3) = 8 + 12 = 20**. The right strip has 3 cells *in each of four rows*, not just 3 cells total. | Counting only one row; reconstruct 4×5 grid and recount disjoint tiles. |
| S7 | One half of the **same 12-cell whole** = **6 squares**, one fourth = **3 squares**. Each drawing must partition a 12-cell rectangle into 2 regions of 6 or 4 regions of 3 (for example straight equal-width column strips in a 3×4 grid where possible). | Shading 6 arbitrary squares with no two equal regions is insufficient; use explicit equal-area boundaries. |
| S8 | **16 ÷ 4 = 4** identical-sized squares per fourth. Example: four 1-column strips in a 4×4 grid, each containing 4 squares; all 16 are included and none overlaps. | Four pieces sized 2, 3, 5, 6 are not four equal fourths even though sum is 16. |
| S9 | **One half > one fourth of identical 8-square wholes** because the shaded areas are **4 squares > 2 squares**, with both wholes 8 equal-area squares. | If wholes differ, 1/2 and 1/4 alone cannot determine a numeric cell comparison; keep wholes fixed. |
