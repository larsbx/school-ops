# Episode 3 — Same whole, equally sized halves and fourths (teacher-only draft)

**Status:** DRAFT, not ready. **Local goal:** `G3M-P3`. **Primary art:** arithmetic with equal-area geometric partitions; not a replacement for Grade 2 equal-group/sharing or a comprehensive fractions course. **Prerequisites:** E3 sharing and E4 equal cell areas demonstrated; no assumed prior fraction notation mastery. **Materials:** 20 equal-area paper tiles or tactile strips in equal cells. **Proposed time:** 25 min.

| Activity | Minutes |
| --- | ---: |
| Grammar of whole, half, fourth, equal area | 5 |
| Logic/model and critique | 7 |
| Guided construction and fading | 6 |
| Rhetoric / independent exit | 5 |
| Feedback/retry | 2 |
| **Total** | **25** |

**Grammar (5):** A **whole** is the explicitly chosen collection or area. A **half** means one of two **equal** shares; a **fourth/quarter** means one of four equal shares. Use 1/2 and 1/4 to *name the shares* of this declared whole; the number below the fraction bar names how many equal parts compose the whole. Do not teach a general rule for comparing unlike wholes.

**Logic/model (7):** Draw a 3-by-4 rectangle of **12 equal squares**. Color six squares as one half and three squares as one fourth in **separate copies of the same-sized whole**. Then ask: can a drawing with four pieces of areas 1, 2, 4 and 5 squares call each piece “one fourth”? No; 1+2+4+5=12 but the four areas differ. A correct total alone does not prove fair shares.

**Guided (6):** With two 4×4 squares grids, partition 16 identical cells into two equal regions (8 each) and four equal regions (4 each). Request two different geometric partitions where feasible, with **equal areas**, not necessarily identical outlines. Ask which has more cells, a half or a fourth of *the same 16-cell whole*: 8 > 4. If "one fourth" is written for a random four-cell patch, ask the learner to show **all four** equal parts covering the whole.

**Rhetoric / exit X3 (5):** Use a **20-cell whole**. Explain how many equal-size cells form a half (**10**) and one fourth (**5**), and why a half of that whole is larger (**10 > 5**). Draw equal partition borders or describe a valid layout. If an answer merely says 10 and 5 without equal partition evidence and the same-whole condition, the criterion is unmet.

**Feedback (2):** If learner compares a half of 8 cells to a quarter of 20 by fraction labels alone, point out different whole sizes: 4 versus 5 cells. Reassess with another fixed 12-cell whole split evenly into 3 or 4 groups after a physical demonstration. Preserve accessible response modes and do not grade the speed of drawing.
