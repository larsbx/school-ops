# Lesson 2 — Reversing an if–then rule (teacher-facing draft)

**Status: draft/not ready.** Provisional **25 minutes**; local goals `G3R-P1`, `G3R-P2`. Entry: accurate E3 counterexample or remedial recheck; Lesson 1 completed as instruction, **not** mastery. ## Trivium and the actual investigation

- **Grammar:** distinguish the **condition** and **consequence** of “if blue, then dot”; a **converse** switches them to “if dot, then blue.”
- **Logic:** R1 is true within A–F; its converse is **false** because D is dotted and red. R3's converse “if numbered, then striped” is separately false because E has a number but no stripe.
- **Rhetoric:** explain an objection using the form “This converse is false because card ___ has ___ but not ___”; invite peer to verify in two cells.

## Proposed 25-minute teaching sequence

| Phase | Minutes | Usable teacher and learner actions |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Revisit prerequisite | 4 | Say R1, point to the **blue** condition and **dot** consequence; read A and B entries aloud. |
| Model reversal | 6 | Place two arrows on board: blue → dot and dot → blue. Ask whether they express the same rule. Verify D by its **red** and **dot yes** cells. |
| Guided examples | 7 | Learners attempt P3 and P5, then P4 to contrast a **supported** forward rule. Teacher asks whether E refutes R3 or only its converse. |
| Explain and rebut | 5 | Pairs present one valid rule and one false converse, then swap which two cells a partner must verify. No single positive example may validate a universal. |
| Independent exit X2 | 3 | Ask: “A learner claims **if numbered, then triangle** for this packet. Test the claim with one card; say why.” |

**Teacher exit key X2:** **False**; **E** has **Number = yes** but **Form = square**. A, C and F support the claim but cannot rescue it from E. This is a different inverse from R3's converse and still needs an explicit counterexample.

**Feedback:** If a learner calls E a counterexample to R3 itself, ask them to restate R3 as **stripe → number**, show E lacks the antecedent stripe, and retry on a new statement. If condition and consequence are confused, model with verbal “when this happens, that follows,” then check the direction again. [P1–P8 teacher models](../practice-teacher.md).
