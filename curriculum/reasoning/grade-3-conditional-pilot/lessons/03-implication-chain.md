# Lesson 3 — Link rules without adding a link (teacher-facing draft)

**Status: draft/not ready.** Provisional **25 minutes**; local goals `G3R-P2`, `G3R-P3`. Entry: Lessons 1–2 work checked; learner can name a rule's condition and refute a converse. Materials: [R2 and R3 on packet](../rule-cards.md), [P6–P7](../practice-learner.md), labeled arrows/large print or oral reading.

## Trivium and the actual investigation

- **Grammar:** `premise`, `conclusion`, `chain`, and an arrow showing a rule's direction. A premise is something provided; a conclusion is what follows from premises.
- **Logic:** R2 says **triangle → stripe**. R3 says **stripe → number**. Therefore **triangle → number** for all A–F. The reverse **number → stripe** fails at E; **dot → number** fails at B or D.
- **Rhetoric:** speak or diagram the two links in order, name both premises, and challenge any unlicensed extra arrow.

## Proposed 25-minute teaching sequence

| Phase | Minutes | Usable teacher and learner actions |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Review arrows | 4 | Read R2 and R3 precisely, pairing the same word **stripe** at the end of R2/start of R3. |
| Build a complete chain | 6 | Place three labeled pieces triangle → stripe → number. Show A, C and F as corroborating rows; teach why two valid links support the compound implication. |
| Guided error search | 7 | Learners solve P6–P7. Compare dot → number with B and D. Ask whether R1 supplies the *missing* dot → stripe link (it does not). |
| Reciprocal explanation | 5 | Partners check each other's chain by stating the **premise for each arrow**. Invite a counterexample to “number → triangle”: E. |
| Independent exit X3 | 3 | Ask: “Explain why every **triangle in A–F** is numbered using two rules; then say why this does **not** mean every numbered card is a triangle.” |

**Teacher exit key X3:** R2 gives **triangle → stripe**; R3 gives **stripe → number**; hence triangle → number. E is **numbered** and **square**, so the attempted converse fails. Require **both** premises and **E's two relevant fields**.

**Feedback:** For an unsupported chain, have learner write each premise beside its arrow and leave a **gap** rather than inventing a connecting rule. If the learner treats corroborating examples as a universal proof beyond A–F, restate the packet's closed scope. [Guided key](../practice-teacher.md).
