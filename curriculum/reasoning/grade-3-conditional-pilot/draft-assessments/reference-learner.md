# Grade 3 reasoning — independent reference form (learner; draft)

**Public exemplar, not ready or secure assessment.** Use the full [original six-card ledger and R1–R3](../rule-cards.md) as an allowed premise sheet. Consider **only these six cards** unless a task explicitly discusses a different scope. Answer independently: no key, feedback, peer prompts or live teacher hints. **Untimed**; teacher may read prompts and table aloud, enlarge or provide tactile/text rows, and transcribe dictated or AAC answers without supplying logical choices. You may explain by words, labeled arrows or pointed rows.

**Q1 (G3R-P1).** Name **every blue card** and explain how you know there are no others in this packet.

**Q2 (G3R-P1).** Decide: “Every card with a dot is blue.” If false, give **one counterexample**, citing its Dot and Color fields.

**Q3 (G3R-P1).** Is “every triangle is striped” true in the packet? Name **all the triangles** and report their Stripe fields.

**Q4 (G3R-P2).** Rewrite R1 as an **if–then** sentence. Mark the condition and the consequence.

**Q5 (G3R-P2).** Write the **converse** of R1, then decide whether that converse is true in this packet. Support your answer with a card.

**Q6 (G3R-P3).** A peer says “every triangle in this packet has a number.” Explain whether this follows from the supplied rules by naming **both** rule steps and the final conclusion.

**Q7 (G3R-P2).** A peer reverses R3: “if a card has a number, it has a stripe.” Test the claim and give a counterexample if false.

**Q8 (G3R-P3).** A peer says, “B is dotted, so it must have a number.” Does the conclusion follow? Refer to B's actual fields and the **missing premise**.

**Q9 (G3R-P3).** Without adding any information, can the packet establish whether a **new seventh card G** will have a dot? Say **unknown, yes, or no**, justify, and identify evidence that would settle it.

This is not a test of writing speed, hearing, color vision, or any real student's personal traits. Do not record learner responses here.
