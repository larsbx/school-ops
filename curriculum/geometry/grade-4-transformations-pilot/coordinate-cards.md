# Learner coordinate packet — original fictional boards

**Draft / not ready.** These cards are publicly visible teaching inputs, never unseen test forms. The board has **six columns 0–5 left to right** and **six rows 0–5 top to bottom**. A cell `(c,r)` is a whole unit square. Keep the board's original labels after each move: **do not rotate the label numbers**. Every filled cell is one square unit of area. Perimeter counts outer exposed cell sides in length units; a shared full side is not exposed.

- **V**, vertical mirror: axis between columns 2 and 3; pair column 0 with 5, 1 with 4, 2 with 3. A cell keeps its row.
- **H**, horizontal mirror: axis between rows 2 and 3; pair row 0 with 5, 1 with 4, 2 with 3. A cell keeps its column.
- **Q**, clockwise quarter-turn about the center: `(c,r) → (5-r,c)`. A quarter-turn is not the same move as V.
- Write transformed shapes as sets of coordinate pairs. Order of listing does not matter. Two squares share a side only if their rows agree and their columns differ by one, **or** their columns agree and their rows differ by one.

**Card A, four connected cells:** `(0,1), (1,1), (1,2), (1,3)`.

**Card B, four-cell bar:** `(1,2), (2,2), (3,2), (4,2)`.

**Card C, three-cell corner:** `(1,1), (1,2), (2,2)`.

**Card D, 2×2 block:** `(1,1), (2,1), (1,2), (2,2)`.

**Card E, four-cell row:** `(1,1), (2,1), (3,1), (4,1)`.

Read a pair aloud as "column c, row r"; when no diagram is available, the coordinate list itself is the complete accessible representation. These are *definitions*, not a displayed answer key. To reason about a new transformation, apply its rule to each cell and verify that all image cells remain inside the grid.
