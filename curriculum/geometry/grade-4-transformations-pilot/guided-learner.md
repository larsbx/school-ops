# Guided coordinate-and-proof tasks — learner

**Draft, publicly exposed coaching material.** Use [the coordinate packet](coordinate-cards.md), 6×6 board and unit-square convention. Show your transformed coordinates **and** a reason; use speech/touch/dictation if preferred. The teacher keeps the worked key on a separate, teacher-only surface.

**G1.** Find V`(1,2)`. Which coordinate remains unchanged?

**G2.** Map each cell of Card A across V. How many filled squares are in the image? Make no assumption that the image has the same location.

**G3.** Apply V twice to `(0,4)`. Explain what changes and what returns.

**G4.** Apply V to the entire Card B. Is the image the same **set** of squares as B? Must each cell stay in its original position?

**G5.** Rotate `(1,4)` clockwise by Q about the board center. Name the new cell and how the new row was found.

**G6.** Find all four Q-image cells of Card A. Explain why this is not V(A).

**G7.** Compare Card A and Q(A). Calculate area and perimeter of each. Justify why a quarter-turn preserves contact between full sides.

**G8.** A learner says, "Any two shapes made of four unit squares must have the same perimeter." Compare Cards A and D: give both areas and perimeters with units, and refute or defend the claim using shared sides. Explain how this claim differs from "reflecting or turning a whole shape preserves its area and perimeter."
