# Grade 4 geometry — exposed reference tasks (learner)

**Draft/reference only; NOT an unseen assessment or evidence of mastery.** Untimed illustrative prompts. Use [original coordinate rules and Cards A–E](../coordinate-cards.md), a 6×6 labeled board, movable tokens and writing or oral/tactile response as needed. State the coordinates, units and reasons requested. Teacher keys are separate. A new unseen teacher-reviewed form is needed for real assessment.

**S1.** Under V, where does `(2,5)` go? State which coordinate is unchanged.

**S2.** Under H, where does `(4,0)` go? State which coordinate is unchanged.

**S3.** Reflect the two-cell set `{(0,0),(1,0)}` under V. Give both output coordinates. Why are no squares lost?

**S4.** Does the entire Card B have V symmetry? Show the correspondence of the four columns; explain the difference between an unchanged shape and an individually unmoved square.

**S5.** Under Q, where does `(0,2)` go? Explain why the new column equals that number.

**S6.** Apply Q to `{(1,4),(2,4)}`. State the output set.

**S7.** Reflect Card C under V. Compute both the original and reflected area and perimeter, with units; defend your result by counting shared sides.

**S8.** A classmate claims moving every cell of `{(0,1),(0,2),(1,2)}` one column to the right is **the V reflection** of the original. Construct both images and decide whether the claim holds.

**S9.** Refute or defend: "Every four-cell shape has perimeter 10 length units." Compare Cards A and D, using a reason based on full shared sides.

**S10.** Start with `(1,1)`, reflect under V, then apply Q to the result. Give both intermediate and final coordinates, label the operations in order and explain one property that both moves preserve. Would reversing the order necessarily give the same result for this point? Show it.
