# Transfer clinic T1–T5 — learner tasks (optional, draft)

**Record:** original, publicly exposed **coached practice**, not a secure assessment or evidence of mastery. These five tasks extend the [four core episodes](README.md) only after relevant entry checks; they are **not extra required outcomes or a registered lesson**. Leave notes and any individualized response records in private school systems.

For T1–T4, each `#` is a filled square with side **one length unit**, each `.` is empty. Trace full **exposed tile sides**; two tiles meeting only at a corner do not share a side. Explain your answer by showing a model, drawing, raised-tile trace, or speech. Square-unit area and length-unit boundary must not be interchanged.

## T1 — A courtyard opening

A rectangular patch contains an empty middle square:

```text
###
#.#
###
```

(a) How many filled square units are present? (b) Trace the *outer outline* and report its length. (c) Trace the four sides facing the middle empty square and report that length separately. (d) If a request says “count **every exposed side** of the tiled region,” what is the combined length? Why are (b) and (d) not the same request? The opening is a fictional grid example, not a proposed real construction.

## T2 — Corners do not join edges

Compare arrangement A and arrangement B:

```text
A:    B:
#.    ##
.#
```

Find the area and total exposed-side boundary for each. Which arrangement qualifies if the instruction requires **one region joined by full tile sides**? Explain why A's two corners do not conceal any edges.

## T3 — A hooked six-tile path

```text
###
#..
##.
```

Trace area and exposed-side boundary. Compare your answers with a **2-by-3 rectangle** (`### / ###`) and a **1-by-6 strip** (`######`). Someone says: “We checked three shapes, therefore the rectangle is best among *every possible* six-tile design.” Accept, reject, or narrow that conclusion. State a claim that the shown evidence **does** justify.

## T4 — Turn the page, not the geometry

Compare a 2-by-3 filled rectangle (`### / ###`) with a 3-by-2 filled rectangle (`## / ## / ##`). Are their square-unit areas and exposed length-unit boundaries equal? Does turning a rectangular drawing by a quarter-turn change its amount or boundary? Explain with equal-square counting **and** a side trace rather than appearance alone.

## T5 — When the tile size changes (optional stretch)

Two rectangular models each arrange **four tiles, two rows and two columns**. In model C each tile is a square of side **1 cm**. In model D each tile is a square of side **2 cm**. The 2 cm squares have four 1 cm-by-1 cm squares inside each tile. No gaps or overlaps.

(a) Report the **number of tiles** in both models. (b) Report the *physical* area in square centimetres and the *outer boundary* in centimetres for each model. (c) Reply to “equal tile counts always mean equal floor areas.” Which assumption changed from T1–T4?

**Access:** tactile squares with a removable centre tile, raised boundaries, enlarged text or diagrams, read-aloud and dictated/scribed explanations are alternatives to vision- or pencil-dependent answers. Work without a stopwatch. No correct result is inferred from manipulating a tile without explaining the requested distinction.
