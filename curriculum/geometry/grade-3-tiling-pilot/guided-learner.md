# Guided tasks G1–G7 — learner copy (draft; coached practice)

All `#` are equal filled squares, `.` are empty; every tile side has length one length unit. Support your number with a trace, reason or spoken explanation.

**G1** Two rows `### / ###`: count the square tiles (area) and the outside unit edges (perimeter). Use **different correct units**.

**G2** One row `######`: compute area and perimeter. Compare both quantities to G1. Why can they differ?

**G3** A `####` strip and the G1 2×3 rectangle: test “equal perimeter always means equal area” by recording *both* quantities for *both* shapes.

**G4** The non-rectangular shape:
```text
##.
###
```
Compute area and perimeter without including the dot. Explain which shared edges disappear from the boundary.

**G5** At a shared vertex O the first ray is **east**. Three second rays are northeast, north, northwest. Classify each anticlockwise opening from east as *less than*, *equal to*, or *greater than* a square-corner (right) angle. Explain.

**G6** Model courtyard: exactly six equal square tiles, with either G1 (2×3) or G2 (1×6) as the offered layout. Which choice has the **shorter outside boundary**? State each area/perimeter; respond to “the longer-looking strip has more floor.” Do not claim all shapes have been compared.

**G7** A right angle drawn with rays 2 units long is redrawn with rays 5 units long in **the same directions**. Has the angle grown? Test by a fixed-vertex paper/tactile hinge or a verbal description.

These are accessible, coached learning tasks, **not assessment results**. Teacher answers live in a separate teacher-only document.
