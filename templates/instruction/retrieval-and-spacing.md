# Template — Retrieve, check, correct, revisit later

- Pattern: retrieval-and-spacing, version 1
- Source: [IES/WWC practice guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/practiceguide/1);
  [evidence limits](../../docs/instructional-pattern-sources.md)
- Status: reusable draft template and original draft exemplar
- Fill the [common contract](README.md#common-authoring-contract).

## Copy and fill

1. **Already taught content:** [named relationship/component and last teaching context].
2. **Retrieval task:** [short actual prompt; no visible worked answer; declared objects/support].
3. **Check and explain:** [key plus reason/model; learner checks the meaning].
4. **Repair:** [brief targeted model if needed, followed by a new attempt].
5. **Later revisit:** [another teaching occasion after a gap; new keyed prompt].
6. **Mix deliberately:** [only previously taught relationships, with each criterion visible].

The author proposes review occasions, not a new automation or an invented learner
schedule. Choose intervals from the actual teaching plan. Retrieval is not a
timed drill, and early difficulty is information for teaching rather than a
failure label. Explain and correct after an attempt instead of reinforcing errors
through repeated unchecked guessing.

## Original Grade 2 exemplar

C-MATH-001 / U-MATH-001-01 / L-MATH-001-01-01; O-MATH-001-001, units/exchange/zero;
0–99. Entry: these relationships have been taught. Counters/read-aloud allowed,
with their use stated consistently. Propose a 3–5-minute review block on each
selected teaching occasion; no dates or fixed delay are asserted.

| Occasion/task | Prompt | Key and reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First review, Q1 | How many single counted ones form one ten? Show it. | 10; ten of the named one-unit form the grouped unit |
| First review, Q2 | Unpack one ten from 34 and explain | 3 tens + 4 ones = 2 tens + 14 ones |
| Later occasion, Q3 | Unpack one ten from 47 and explain | 4 tens + 7 ones = 3 tens + 17 ones |
| Same later occasion, Q4 | Build 80 and explain zero ones | 8 tens + 0 ones; no extra ones beyond the grouped tens |
| Another fresh occasion, Q5 | Pack 1 ten and 15 ones into complete tens | 2 tens + 5 ones = 25; ten singles become one ten |

Use Q5 only after packing as well as unpacking has been taught.
If Q3 loses ten, briefly reconstruct a different physical example (for instance,
22 = 2 tens + 2 ones = 1 ten + 12 ones), then choose a fresh keyed task.
That corrected attempt is supported teaching evidence.

Grammar names units, logic explains equal-valued exchanges, and rhetoric makes
the reconstruction intelligible. Judge each response and support level; do not
infer retention, all of outcome 001 or transfer to hundreds from a short review.
Keep learner evidence private; the shared plan records proposed curriculum changes.
