## Copy and fill

1. **Engage:** [concrete puzzle that activates a relevant idea; observe informal conceptions].
2. **Explore:** [safe, bounded manipulation/observation with a question and adequate materials].
3. **Explain:** [learner offers an account; teacher makes the mathematical relation explicit].
4. **Elaborate:** [apply the relation in another achievable case].
5. **Evaluate:** [fresh model/reason task plus feedback and a next step].

This is a sequence that can span several lessons; do not compress it into five
token activities. Exploration has a mathematical purpose and teacher guidance.
The engage conversation is not automatically an independent pre-test.
State what must already be known and supply instruction if an entry skill is absent.

## Original Grade 2 measurement exemplar

C-MATH-001 / U-MATH-001-04 (currently an outline); O-MATH-001-008, chosen-unit
measurement component. This is an original authoring example, not an existing
ready lesson or a new lesson ID.
Entry: count to twelve, compare aligned lengths and place equal units end-to-end
without gaps or overlaps. Materials prepared by an adult: a 12 cm strip, an
8 cm strip, equal 1 cm short units and equal 2 cm long units, blank recording
paper. Centimeter dimensions specify preparation; the child may name units
“short” and “long.” Check the actual cut lengths before teaching.
Propose two 25-minute sessions with flexibility; no observation results are claimed.

| Phase | Actual task | Teacher key/evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Engage | Two people report 12 short units and 6 long units for the same strip. Can both counts describe it? | Invite a test; do not judge the opening guess as assessed understanding |
| Explore | Measure the 12 cm strip using each unit, aligned from the same end with no gaps/overlaps | 12 short units; 6 long units |
| Explain | Show how two short units match one long unit. Why did the count change? | 2 short units = 1 long unit; strip length is unchanged, chosen unit differs |
| Elaborate | Predict and then measure the 8 cm strip with both units | 8 short units; 4 long units; verify with actual placement |
| Evaluate | Given a strip covered by 10 short units, show how many of these long units cover it and justify | 5 long units; each covers the same length as 2 shorts |

The final task supplies the short-unit measurement; it checks conversion and
reasoning, not an unobserved full practical-measurement performance.
To assess that practical component, independently inspect a fresh physical
measurement as well. A correct count with gaps/overlaps is not acceptable measurement.

Grammar: unit, length, equal and labels. Logic: same magnitude, different count
because unit sizes differ. Rhetoric: diagram or demonstrated explanation another
person can check. Write “12 short units = 6 long units,” not the false
unit-free statement “12 = 6.”

This math adaptation makes no general effectiveness claim for Grade 2.
It does not add science standards or fabricate astronomical observations.
Complete the actual unit/lesson/assessment records and reviews before teaching use.
