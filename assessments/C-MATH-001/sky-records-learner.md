# U-MATH-001-05 — sky-record literacy and date intervals: learner reference form (DRAFT)

**Audience:** intended Grade 2 after demonstrated Unit 5 entry work and teaching; this public form is **not a secure unseen assessment**. The course is C-MATH-001, and the existing outcome components under consideration are **O-MATH-001-010** and **O-MATH-001-011**. **No new assessment ID** is assigned here.

**Conditions/materials:** untimed; original invented record table, blank date strip, empty equally scaled chart grid or tactile counters. A teacher may read labels and scribe speech, and learners may show models by pointing, AAC, tactile or enlarged chart. Do **not** give the reason, pre-count the day intervals, show the teacher key or withdraw legitimate access supports. The optional real-data/source task at the end **cannot be completed** from the fictional table. These examples are original, freely exposed and require unseen variants before independent summative use.

**Crucial: the dates and observations below are FICTIONAL TRAINING DATA**. Nothing here states how the Moon actually appeared on any of these dates. “No” means **not seen in that view**, not absent from the sky.

## A. Words, records and limits

**S5-01 (O011).** Classify each as a **report in the fictional record** or a **prediction not established by this record**: (a) “The log says the Moon was seen in the view at 16:00 on May 5.” (b) “The Moon will be visible from this spot every Tuesday.” Give one reason for each classification.

**S5-03 (O010).** On May 6 the view was blocked. Can the row establish that the Moon was absent from the sky? Name what the table actually records and what is unknown.

**S5-07 (O010/O011).** May 5 and May 16 each say “yes, seen.” A classmate claims, “We have proved an **11-day Moon-appearance cycle**.” Does the table justify this conclusion? Explain what was measured, what was **not** measured, and what more would be needed to test a physical repeat.

**S5-08 (O011).** Write or say two different, precise sentences, one about May 13 (“clear view, not seen”) and one about May 6 (“view blocked, not checked”). Do not turn either into an unsupported assertion about the whole sky.

## B. Day intervals and calendar conventions

**S5-02 (O010).** Mark May 5, 6, 7, 8 and 9 on a strip. How many **one-day gaps** are between May 5 and May 9? State the counting unit and explain why the number of labels differs.

**S5-05 (O010).** Count the **day intervals** from May 6 to May 13. Explain whether that interval is a conventional **one-week** span and whether the log proves that the Moon looked the same at the two endpoints.

## C. Table and diagram

**S5-04 (O011).** Make a three-row summary table from the five records, using the Moon-record states **seen**, **not seen in clear view**, and **not checked because view blocked**. Give a count for each category and check the total.

**S5-06 (O011).** Make two equal-scale bars (or tactile rows of identical units) for **clear-view records** and **blocked-view records**. Label both and write each count. Say why these bars do **not** measure the number of actual Moon appearances in the sky.

## D. Genuine dated-record transfer (separately gated)

**S5-09 (O010 physical sky evidence).** The teacher must first make an **independently verified, accessible, safely dated source or observation opportunity** available. No such packet is supplied by this reference form. On a **separate private blank record**, identify two genuine records on distinct dates, their source/time/conditions, calculate the elapsed day intervals, compare only supported features, and state one limit of the claim. If viewing or source access was blocked, record **unassessed** and seek a safe reviewed alternative; **never invent** an entry, copy this fictional table as a real observation, require night travel or photograph a student.
