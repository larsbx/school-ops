## Complete model evidence

### U6-01
Seven tens and six ones plus five tens and eight ones: 6 + 8 = 14 ones, exchange ten for one ten and four ones; 7 + 5 + 1 = 13 tens, exchange ten for one hundred and three tens. **134 counters altogether**; 134 − 58 = 76. Counting-unit exchange does not add or delete objects.

### U6-02
**76 unmarked spaces** because 67 + 76 = 143. A counted-on model is 67→70 (+3), 70→100 (+30), 100→143 (+43); total 3 + 30 + 43 = 76. Counted unit is spaces, not books or counters.

### U6-03
**48 paper shapes remain**. Taking away changes 96 by −48; the question asks for the remaining whole count. 48 + 48 = 96 checks. An addition would answer a different question; no equal containers have been mentioned.

### U6-04
Start **70**, advance **25** to **95**, then retreat **13** to **82**. Mark 70, 95 and 82 at uniform one-unit distances or label ten-unit anchor segments with appropriately subdivided single units; the length of a move is number of equal steps, not number of labels drawn. Since 82 + 13 = 95, backward move is correct.

### U6-05
Table: Day A 14, Day B 9, Day C 16 **stamps**; 14 + 9 + 16 = **39 stamps total**. Day C exceeds Day B by **7 stamps**. Day D is **unknown from the given records**; 23 may turn out true or false, but it was neither recorded nor entailed. These are fictional task numbers, not empirical astronomy evidence.

### U6-06
Plan A: 3 packs × 9 cards = **27**, so **1 extra** above needed 26. Plan B: 2 packs × 14 = **28**, **2 extras**. **Both sufficient; A** leaves fewer extras. The task does **not** give prices, so it cannot justify cheapest. Fewer packs by itself is not the optimization target.
