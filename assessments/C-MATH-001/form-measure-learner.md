Each response must include the answer **and a reason or demonstration**. “Row × column” is an optional way to count, not a prerequisite formula: counting each identical square is equally valid. All rectangles and grids described below are filled with **identically sized unit squares**, with no gaps or overlaps.

## A. Forms and equal square counts (O012)

**G4-01.** Shape A has **3 rows of 3** unit squares. Shape B has **1 row of 9** same-size unit squares. State the count in each. Is “different outline means different covered square count” true? Explain with these shapes.

**G4-02.** Shape C is **2 rows of 2** equal squares; shape D is **1 row of 4** of the *same* squares. Which shape is a square and which is a nonsquare rectangle? Do they use the same number of unit squares? Explain why equal tile count does not force the same outline.

## B. Chosen unit and repeated length (O008)

**G4-03.** One straight strip is exactly **14 short units** long. Cover it from one endpoint to the other with 1-short-unit rods. How many rods? Cover it instead with 2-short-unit rods. How many? Name both units and explain why the counts differ although the strip did not change.

**G4-04.** A second strip is exactly **12 short units** long. Lee lays **five 2-short-unit rods** end-to-end from the starting endpoint, leaving an uncovered section at the far end. How many *short units* remain uncovered? How many double rods **in total** would cover the strip without gaps? Explain why Lee's five-rod count is not a complete measure.

## C. Even number-line gaps and movement (O009)

**G4-05.** On a line with **equal gaps** and tick labels 0 through 12, begin at tick **2**, travel **7 gaps forward**, then **3 gaps backward**. What is the final tick? Trace or name the intervals; do not count the initial tick as one of the forward steps.

**G4-06.** On the same line, what is the distance in **unit gaps** from tick **3** to tick **11**? A friend says “9, because I can count both endpoint labels.” Check the claim with an interval-by-interval explanation.

## D. Equal partition of one tiled whole (O012)

**G4-07.** One whole is a **4-row, 4-column** grid of identical squares. Draw a vertical divider **after column 2**. Count the squares on each side, state the whole's square count and explain why the two shares are equal.

**G4-08.** Another whole is **2 rows and 7 columns** of identical squares. A divider after column **3** gives two pieces. Count each piece and decide whether they are equal shares. If not, find a way to split this same whole into **two equal-square shares**, give both counts and explain the change.
