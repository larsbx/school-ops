## Complete answers and worked evidence

### U3-01 — Equal groups
Four distinct groups, five counters in each, **20 counters total**. Every counter is included exactly once; 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 = 20. Answer units are *groups*, *counters per group*, and *counters in all*. Just a product with no model/reason is incomplete.

### U3-02 — Changing an array
Both **3 rows of 6** and **6 rows of 3** contain **18 counters**. The very same objects were rearranged; nothing was inserted or lost. A correct response distinguishes the two descriptions yet argues why the count is conserved.

### U3-03 — How many in each?
**4 counters per pot**, with four disjoint pots; 4 + 4 + 4 + 4 = **16**. The answer is the size of each pot's collection, **not** the number of pots. Recombination checks the share.

### U3-04 — How many groups?
**6 groups**, each of 3 counters; 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = **18**. The answer counts *groups* because the group *size* was fixed at 3, unlike U3-03.

### U3-05 — Double and halve
**Double 9 = 18** via two sets of 9: 9 + 9 = 18. Half that total is **9 counters**; reconstruction 9 + 9 = 18 shows the inverse. Both directions and a model are required.

### U3-06 — Test a claim
False **for whole counters**: two equal groups of 8 use 16 counters, leaving **1**; 8 + 8 + 1 = **17**. Groups of 9 and 8 use all objects but are **not equal**. This does not assert that 17 has no fractional half.
