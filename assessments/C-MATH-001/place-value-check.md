# Place-value unit check

- Assessment ID: A-MATH-001-02
- Course/unit: [C-MATH-001 / U-MATH-001-01](../../curriculum/mathematics/grade-2/units/place-value/README.md)
- Intended grades: 2nd grade
- Purpose: end-of-unit evidence for outcomes 001–004
- Status: draft
- Estimated time: 15–20 minutes, with extra time or separate sittings if needed
- Materials: paper, pencil, counters or base-ten blocks; no calculator
- Support: directions read aloud; spoken/drawn explanations allowed; no answer cues
- Review: pending in [PR #1](https://github.com/larsbx/school-ops/pull/1)

## Choose the taught range

This version provides three-digit tasks and a 1,000 boundary extension. Use it
after those ideas have been taught. For an initial smaller range, adapt quantities
and units to the taught work, keep the reasoning demand, and record the larger
extensions as pending. Evidence from smaller numbers does not establish the full
prepared outcome range.

## Tasks and teacher key

Original school-ops tasks. Keep the teacher-key column separate when presenting
the tasks to a learner. Assess understanding with fresh examples if a task has
already been practiced.

| Item | Task | Teacher key |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Model 462 and identify the amounts contributed by each digit. | 4 hundreds, 6 tens, 2 ones; 400 + 60 + 2 |
| 2 | Exchange ten groups of ten for a larger unit. What amount is unchanged? | One hundred; 10 tens = 100 |
| 3 | Model 308 and 200. Explain the zero positions. | 3 hundreds, 0 tens, 8 ones; 2 hundreds, 0 tens, 0 ones |
| 4 | Count by ones from 298 through 302. | 298, 299, 300, 301, 302 |
| 5 | Starting at 95, give five terms while counting by fives. | 95, 100, 105, 110, 115 |
| 6 | Give five terms by tens from 180, then five by hundreds from 500. | 180, 190, 200, 210, 220; 500, 600, 700, 800, 900 |
| 7 | Read 604 and express it as a sum of place values. | Six hundred four; 600 + 4 (600 + 0 + 4 also accepted) |
| 8 | Write eight hundred fifty as a numeral and a sum of place values. | 850; 800 + 50 |
| 9 | Write and name the amount formed by ten hundreds. | 1,000; one thousand; ten hundreds or one thousand-group |
| 10 | Insert a symbol: 527 __ 572. Explain. | <; tens differ after the equal hundreds |
| 11 | Insert a symbol: 406 __ 390. Explain. | >; 4 hundreds exceed 3 hundreds |
| 12 | Insert a symbol: 630 __ 630. Explain. | =; both amounts are identical |

## Outcome criteria

These are proposed local teaching criteria, adjusted to the taught range.
For a multi-part item, check every part. Explain with words, objects, or drawings.

| Course outcome | Items | Proposed evidence to progress |
| --- | --- | --- |
| O-MATH-001-001 | 1–3 | All three correct; explain the ten-tens exchange and what a zero position means |
| O-MATH-001-002 | 4–6 | All sequences correct, including boundary crossings; describe the repeated step |
| O-MATH-001-003 | 7–9 | All conversions correct, including the 1,000 boundary; connect one numeral to its place-value model |
| O-MATH-001-004 | 10–12 | All comparisons and symbols correct; justify the first differing place or equality |

The boundary 1,000 has four digits; compare-three-digit tasks use values through
999. Do not treat 1,000 as a three-digit hundreds/tens/ones numeral.

Across each outcome, inspect grammar (names and representations), logic (the reason),
and rhetoric (an explanation another person can follow). Permit objects, drawings,
and speech so that written expression does not obscure understanding.

When evidence is incomplete, revisit the relevant lesson and reassess with changed
numbers. Record individual evidence privately and use the pattern to revise pacing.
